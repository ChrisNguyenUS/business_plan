A bank account is the first step to getting paid, paying rent and starting to build financial standing in the U.S. Federal rules set a minimum of information a **bank must collect**, but **each bank or credit union has its own policy** on which documents it accepts. This article is about personal accounts. Business accounts have different requirements; see [Opening a Business Bank Account](/en/blog/business-bank-account-llc).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Under the federal rule (CIP), a bank must collect **a name, date of birth, address and an identification number**. A non-U.S. person can use an **SSN, an ITIN, a passport number with the country of issuance**, or an alien identification card number.
> - **Banks aren't required to accept every type of document.** Policies differ, so **call and ask first**.
> - To open an account that **earns interest**, you usually need an **SSN or ITIN**. Without one, you may only be able to open a non-interest account.
> - A bank must form a reasonable belief that it **knows who you are**, so it often looks at more than one document.
> - Choose an account with **low fees** and check for **FDIC deposit insurance** (or NCUA for credit unions).

## Documents you usually need

The CFPB, the federal consumer finance agency, has a [checklist for opening a bank account for newcomers](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_checklist_opening_bank_account_web.pdf). In general, prepare:

- **Photo ID:** a passport, driver's license, ID card or green card.
- **A tax identification number:** SSN or ITIN. See [Getting a Social Security Number](/en/blog/get-social-security-number-replace-card) and [Your First U.S. Tax Return](/en/blog/first-us-tax-return-new-immigrants) for the difference between SSN and ITIN.
- **A U.S. address:** a lease, utility bill or mail addressed to you.
- **An opening deposit**, if the bank requires one.

## What the federal rule says

Under the FDIC's guidance on the Customer Identification Program (CIP) rule, for a **non-U.S. person** a bank must collect **at least one of these**: a taxpayer identification number (TIN), a **passport number and country of issuance**, an alien identification card number, or the number of another government-issued document showing nationality or residence and bearing a photograph. An ITIN counts as an identification number. The bank must have procedures to **form a reasonable belief** that it knows the customer's identity.

This rule says banks **may** accept those documents. It doesn't force every bank to accept them. The CFPB notes that some banks and credit unions accept foreign passports or consular IDs (such as Mexico's Matrícula Consular), while others don't. **Ask each one** what it accepts, for example a Vietnamese passport with a visa, or a green card with an ITIN.

## Interest-bearing and non-interest accounts

The CFPB says that to open an account that **pays interest**, you need an **SSN or ITIN**. If you don't have one yet, you may be limited to a non-interest account.

## Choosing an account

- **Checking:** for everyday use, with a debit card.
- **Savings:** to set money aside, usually earning interest.
- **Monthly fees:** ask how to **avoid them**, for example with direct deposit or a minimum balance.
- **Deposit insurance:** the FDIC insures deposits at banks, and NCUA at credit unions. Check before you open.
- **Language and branches:** if you need support in Vietnamese, ask in advance.

## Opening an account from abroad

Many banks **won't open an account for someone who hasn't arrived in the U.S.** Ask the specific bank, because policies vary. If you have an account in Vietnam and have become a U.S. resident, remember to **report foreign accounts** when required. See [FBAR and Form 8938](/en/blog/fbar-form-8938-vietnam-bank-accounts).

## Before you go to the bank

Gather your documents, then **call or check the bank's website** to learn exactly what it accepts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an SSN to open a U.S. bank account?

Not always. Federal rules let banks use an ITIN or a passport number with the country of issuance for non-U.S. persons. But each bank sets its own policy, and an interest-bearing account usually requires an SSN or ITIN.

### Does a bank accept a Vietnamese passport?

Federal rules allow it, but each bank decides. Call or ask at a branch before you go.

### What if I don't have a permanent address yet?

Banks usually need a U.S. address. A lease or mail addressed to you is common proof. Ask the bank what it accepts.

### Can I open an account with an ITIN?

Under the CIP rule, an ITIN counts as an identification number for opening an account. Each bank still has its own policy, so ask first.

### Is my money safe at a bank?

Deposits at banks are insured by the FDIC and at credit unions by the NCUA, within the insurance program's limits. Check that the institution you choose is insured.

### Will I pay a monthly fee?

It depends on the account. Many banks waive the fee if you have direct deposit or keep a minimum balance. Ask before you open.
