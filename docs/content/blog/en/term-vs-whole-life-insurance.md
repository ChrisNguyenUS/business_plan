Life insurance pays money to your beneficiary when the insured person dies. The two main kinds are **term life**, which covers you for a set number of years, and **permanent life insurance** such as whole life or universal life, which lasts your whole life and builds savings called cash value.

Many immigrant families buy life insurance through a friend's referral, hear "protection and savings in one," and sign without comparing. This guide explains each type using the [Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) life insurance guide](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb018.html), so you know what you are buying.

> **Quick summary**
> - **Term life:** covers 10, 20 or 30 years, level premium during the term, no cash value. For the same death benefit, it usually costs much less than permanent coverage.
> - **Whole life:** covers you for life as long as you pay premiums, with cash value that grows over time.
> - **Universal life:** flexible premiums and death benefit, but the policy can lapse if the cash value runs out.
> - **In Texas:** you get a free-look period of at least 10 to 20 days to cancel for a full refund.
> - **Check the seller:** look up the license on the TDI website or call 1-800-252-3439.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Term life | Whole life | Universal life |
|---|---|---|---|
| How long it covers | A set term (often 10 to 30 years) | Your whole life, if premiums are paid | To a maturity age (often 95 or 100), while cash value remains |
| Premium | Level during the term; rises with age on renewal | Usually level | Adjustable within limits |
| Cash value | None | Yes, grows at a set rate | Yes, rate can change |
| Often fits when | You need large coverage while children are young or a mortgage is open | You want lifelong coverage and accept higher premiums | You want flexibility and will review the policy regularly |

## Term life: coverage for a set time

Term life works like renting coverage for a period. If the insured person dies during the term, the beneficiary receives the death benefit. If the term ends and nothing happens, coverage stops and premiums are not returned.

TDI points to two features to ask about:

- **Renewable:** you can renew at the end of the term without a new medical exam, but the premium is based on your age at renewal and rises sharply.
- **Convertible:** you can switch to permanent coverage without a medical exam, usually up to about age 65.

Many families match the term to the years left on their mortgage, or to when their youngest child will be working.

## Whole life and universal life: lifelong coverage

With permanent insurance, part of each premium goes into **cash value**. It grows slowly and takes years to build. TDI notes:

- **Early withdrawals may carry surrender fees.** Taking out more than the total premiums you paid is usually taxable.
- **Policy loans:** you can borrow against the cash value. If you do not repay the loan, your beneficiary receives less.
- **Universal life** stays in force only while the cash value is $1 or more. Paying low premiums for years can make the policy lapse early, unless it has a no-lapse guarantee and you pay on time.

### Indexed and variable policies

Some permanent policies tie cash value growth to an index such as the S&P 500 (indexed universal life, or IUL), or invest it in stock and bond sub-accounts (variable universal life). Variable cash value can go up or down. In Texas, anyone selling variable life needs both a federal securities license and a state insurance license.

When you review an illustration, ask clearly **which numbers are guaranteed and which are assumptions**. Also ask about the cap rate, monthly charges, and what happens if you pay less than the illustrated premium.

## How much coverage do you need?

TDI suggests starting with:

- **Debts:** mortgage, car loans, credit cards.
- **Income your family would need to replace:** for how many years, and how much each year.
- **Upcoming costs:** children's education, funeral expenses.
- **What you already have:** coverage through work, savings.

Group life insurance through an employer is often only one or two times your yearly salary and **usually ends when you leave the job**. Do not count on it as your family's only protection.

TDI also advises against choosing on price alone. Compare policies with similar coverage from several companies.

## Your rights when you buy in Texas

| Right | What it means |
|---|---|
| Free look | At least 10 to 20 days after you receive the policy. Cancel for any reason and get a full refund |
| Grace period | Most policies give 31 days after a missed premium due date while coverage continues |
| Reinstatement | A lapsed policy can usually be reinstated within 5 years by paying overdue premiums plus interest; you may need a new medical exam |
| Contestable period | For the first 2 years, the company may review your application if the insured dies |

The 2-year contestable period is why you must answer the application **fully and truthfully**, including medical history and tobacco use. TDI says that with wrong or missing information, the company can deny the claim even if the cause of death is unrelated, and only return the premiums.

## Naming beneficiaries

- You can name **more than one beneficiary** and split the benefit by percentage.
- Beneficiaries rarely owe income tax on the death benefit.
- If no beneficiary is living, the money goes to your estate, where heirs may owe taxes and wait through probate.
- Children under 18 usually cannot receive the money directly. Ask how to set it up, for example through a guardian or a trust.
- Update beneficiaries after marriage, divorce or a new child.

## Be careful when someone suggests replacing your policy

Reviewing your coverage every few years is wise, but swapping an old policy for a new one does not always help you. A new policy restarts the 2-year contestable period, cash value builds again from zero, and you may pay surrender fees on the old one. TDI says it is **illegal for an agent to replace a policy just to earn a new commission**.

Before you sign, look up the agent and company on the [TDI website](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) or call the consumer help line at **1-800-252-3439**. If you buy from an unlicensed company and it fails, your family may have no protection.

## Can green card holders or newcomers buy life insurance?

Green card holders can usually buy coverage the same way citizens do. For visa holders and recent arrivals, it depends on the company: some ask about residency status, years in the U.S., or a taxpayer ID (SSN or ITIN). Ask before you apply so you do not waste time on a medical exam.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between term and whole life insurance?

Term life covers a set number of years and has no cash value. Whole life covers you for life if premiums are paid, and builds cash value. For the same death benefit, term usually costs much less.

### Do I get my money back if the term ends and nothing happened?

Usually not. A term policy ends when the term ends. Some policies let you renew or convert to permanent coverage without a new medical exam.

### Can I cancel after I buy?

Yes. In Texas you have a free-look period of at least 10 to 20 days after you receive the policy. Cancel during that time and you get a full refund.

### What is the risk of borrowing against my policy?

If you do not repay the loan, your beneficiary receives a smaller death benefit. Cashing out the entire cash value may cause the company to cancel the policy.

### Is life insurance through my job enough?

Often not. According to TDI, group coverage is usually one or two times your yearly salary and typically ends when you leave the job.

### Does my beneficiary pay tax on the money?

According to TDI, beneficiaries rarely owe income or inheritance tax on the death benefit. If the money goes to your estate instead, heirs may owe taxes.

### How do I check that an agent is licensed?

Look them up on the Texas Department of Insurance website or call 1-800-252-3439. Agents selling variable life also need a federal securities license.
