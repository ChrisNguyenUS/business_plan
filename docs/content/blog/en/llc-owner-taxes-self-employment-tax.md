After forming an LLC, many owners ask: "Does the LLC pay its own tax?" For a **single-member LLC** (one owner), the answer is usually **no**: the LLC has no separate federal income tax return, and its profit **flows onto your personal return**. But you still owe **other taxes**, and you pay them **during the year**, not only in April. This article covers **federal tax** for LLC owners in Texas. The state reporting side (franchise tax) is in [What to Do After Forming a Texas LLC](/en/blog/after-forming-texas-llc).

> **Key takeaways**
> - A **single-member LLC** is by default "disregarded" for income tax: you report the profit on your personal return (usually on Schedule C). A **multi-member LLC** is by default a partnership and files Form 1065.
> - **Self-employment tax** is **15.3%** (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on net self-employment income. You figure it when your net earnings are **$400 or more**.
> - If you expect to owe **$1,000 or more**, you generally must **pay estimated tax** during the year (Form 1040-ES).
> - Starting in 2026, you only need to send a **Form 1099-NEC** when you pay someone **$2,000 or more** (it was $600).
> - Texas has **no personal income tax**, but it has a **6.25% sales tax plus up to 2% local tax** (8.25% maximum) if you sell taxable items.

## How is an LLC taxed federally?

According to the IRS, how an LLC is taxed depends on how many members it has and what you elect:

| Type of LLC | Default | Return |
|---|---|---|
| **One member** | "Disregarded entity": separate from the owner for income tax | Profit reported on your personal return (usually Schedule C) |
| **Multiple members** | **Partnership** | **Form 1065**; each member gets a **Schedule K-1** |
| **Elect to be taxed as a corporation** | Corporation (file Form 8832) | Corporate return |
| **Elect S corporation** | S corporation | Form 1120-S (elected with Form 2553) |

**Note:** the IRS says a single-member LLC is **still treated as a separate entity** for **employment taxes** and certain excise taxes. So if the LLC hires employees, it still handles payroll taxes even though profit goes onto your return.

Should you elect S corporation status? With large profits, it can **sometimes** reduce self-employment tax, but you then have to pay yourself a salary, run payroll and file additional returns. That's a calculation a preparer has to run on your numbers; there is no one-size answer.

## Self-employment tax: the biggest surprise

A single-member LLC owner has **no boss withholding** Social Security and Medicare tax. You pay it yourself, and it's called **self-employment tax**:

- **12.4%** Social Security on net earnings up to the wage cap.
- **2.9%** Medicare, with no cap.
- **Total: 15.3%.**
- **The 2026 Social Security wage cap is $184,500.** For 2027, the Social Security Administration announced on October 14, 2026 a new cap of **$192,300**.

You figure it on **Schedule SE**. When your **net self-employment earnings are $400 or more**, you must calculate it. The IRS lets you **deduct the employer-equivalent portion** of self-employment tax (about half) when figuring adjusted gross income, but that deduction only lowers income tax, not the self-employment tax itself.

*Illustrative example (rounded):* with $60,000 of net profit, self-employment tax is about **$8,478** (15.3% of $55,410, because it's figured on 92.35% of net earnings). That is before federal income tax.

## Estimated tax: pay during the year

Employees have tax withheld every paycheck. LLC owners **don't**, so the IRS expects **quarterly prepayments**:

- You generally must pay if you **expect to owe $1,000 or more** when you file (individuals, including sole proprietors and partners).
- You avoid the penalty if you paid **at least 90% of this year's tax** or **100% of last year's tax**, whichever is smaller. Higher-income taxpayers have special rules in Publication 505.
- Pay with **Form 1040-ES** or online, by phone or in the IRS app.
- There are usually **four installments** a year: around April 15, June 15, September 15 and January 15. For 2026, the last one is due **January 15, 2027**. Check the IRS site for the exact dates each year.

If you also have a job, you can **raise the withholding at work** (Form W-4) instead of paying estimated tax.

## Hiring people: Form 1099-NEC and W-2

- **Independent contractors:** under the new law, you send a **Form 1099-NEC** when you pay someone **$2,000 or more** in the year (for payments made **after December 31, 2025**). Before that the threshold was $600. The IRS says the threshold may be adjusted for inflation starting in 2027.
- **Employees:** you must withhold payroll taxes, pay them in, and send each employee a **W-2** every year. Misclassifying employees as contractors is a common source of penalties.
- **Ask for a W-9** from each contractor when you hire them, so you have their name, address and tax number when it's time to send a 1099.

## Texas state taxes

- **Personal income tax:** none in Texas, according to the Texas Comptroller. An LLC owner in Texas pays no state income tax on profit.
- **Franchise tax and Public Information Report:** due **May 15** each year. The no-tax-due threshold for report years 2026 and 2027 is **$2,650,000** in revenue. Details are in [What to Do After Forming a Texas LLC](/en/blog/after-forming-texas-llc).
- **Sales tax:** Texas charges **6.25%** on all retail sales, plus local tax of **up to 2%**, for a maximum of **8.25%**. You need a **sales tax permit** if you sell taxable goods or services. Reports are filed monthly or quarterly; for monthly filers the due date is the **20th of the following month**.

## Mistakes LLC owners often make

- **Mixing personal and business money** in one account: at tax time you can't tell the expenses apart.
- **Skipping estimated tax** all year and owing a large bill in April, plus penalties.
- **Forgetting self-employment tax** when figuring how much you get to keep.
- **Not keeping receipts**, statements and expense logs. Deductible expenses must be **ordinary and necessary** for the business.
- **Not sending 1099-NEC forms**, or sending them late.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a single-member LLC file its own tax return?

Not by default for federal income tax. The profit goes onto the owner's personal return, usually on Schedule C. The LLC may still owe payroll taxes if it has employees, and it has the Texas reporting.

### How much is self-employment tax?

15.3%: 12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare. You figure it when net self-employment earnings are $400 or more. The 2026 Social Security wage cap is $184,500.

### Do I have to pay estimated tax every quarter?

Usually yes, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more. You avoid the penalty if you paid at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax, whichever is smaller.

### When do I have to send a 1099-NEC?

When you pay a contractor $2,000 or more for payments made after December 31, 2025. Before that the threshold was $600.

### Does Texas have an income tax?

Texas has no personal income tax. It has a franchise tax for businesses (annual report due May 15) and a sales tax on retail sales.

### Does my LLC need a sales tax permit?

If you sell taxable goods or services in Texas, yes. The maximum rate is 8.25%: 6.25% state plus up to 2% local. Monthly filers pay by the 20th of the following month.
