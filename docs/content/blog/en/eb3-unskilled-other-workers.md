**EB-3 Other Workers** is an employment-based green card category, often called "unskilled workers", for jobs that need **less than 2 years of training or experience**, are **full-time**, and are **not temporary or seasonal**. The worker needs a **U.S. employer** to sponsor them: the employer gets a **labor certification (PERM)** from the Department of Labor, then files **Form I-140** with USCIS. When the worker's turn comes in the Visa Bulletin, they interview at a consulate (if abroad, for example in Vietnam) or file Form I-485 (if eligible in the U.S.). **The spouse and unmarried children under 21** can get green cards as derivatives.

Information from the [USCIS EB-3 page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers/employment-based-immigration-third-preference-eb-3) (updated August 20, 2026), checked October 19, 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - For permanent, full-time jobs needing less than 2 years of training or experience.
> - The employer files PERM, then Form I-140; the worker waits for the Visa Bulletin.
> - The spouse and unmarried children under 21 can come too.

## EB-3 has three subcategories

| Subcategory | What the job requires |
|---|---|
| Skilled Workers | At least 2 years of training or experience |
| Professionals | A U.S. bachelor's degree or foreign equivalent |
| **Other Workers** | **Less than 2 years** of training or experience |

In all three, the job must be **a permanent, full-time job in the U.S.**, not temporary or seasonal, and the worker must meet the requirements in the job offer.

## Steps in an EB-3 Other Workers case

1. **The employer requests a prevailing wage** from the Department of Labor for the position.
2. **The employer recruits under the rules** to show no qualified, willing U.S. worker is available.
3. **The employer files PERM** (Form ETA-9089). **PERM** is the Department of Labor's labor certification. According to the [Department of Labor](https://flag.dol.gov/processingtimes), as of October 5, 2026 it was reviewing cases filed in December 2025, averaging **336 days** per case.
4. **The employer files Form I-140** with USCIS, with the approved PERM and proof it can pay the wage.
5. **The worker waits for their turn** in the [Visa Bulletin](/en/blog/eb3-other-workers-wait-time). Other Workers has its own line.
6. **The worker completes the last step:**
   - Abroad: Form DS-260, a medical exam and an interview (for Vietnam, at the U.S. Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City).
   - In the U.S.: Form I-485 if eligible to adjust status. Eligibility depends on current immigration status; ask a licensed immigration attorney.
7. **The worker gets the green card** and starts working for the sponsoring employer.

## How many green cards are there each year?

According to the [October 2026 Visa Bulletin](https://adoption.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin/2027/visa-bulletin-for-october-2026.html), EB-3 gets 28.6% of employment-based visas each year, of which **no more than 10,000 go to Other Workers**. Part of that number is reduced under NACARA; for fiscal year 2027 the reduction is about 150. With so few numbers, Other Workers usually waits longer than Skilled Workers.

## Common industries

Other Workers positions are often in food processing, hotels, restaurants, elder care and manufacturing. The actual job depends on an employer with a real need and an approved PERM.

## What to understand from the start

- **An employer must sponsor you.** There is no EB-3 case without a U.S. employer and PERM (apart from Schedule A jobs such as nursing, which aren't Other Workers jobs).
- **The employer pays PERM costs.** Federal rules bar employers from taking money from workers for PERM. See [EB-3 Costs: Who Pays What](/en/blog/eb3-costs-who-pays).
- **No one can guarantee timing or results.** Timing depends on the Department of Labor, USCIS and the Visa Bulletin. Be wary of "fast track" promises; see [EB-3 Scam Warning Signs](/en/blog/eb3-scam-warning-signs).
- **The job is real.** An EB-3 green card rests on the intent to work for the sponsoring employer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does EB-3 Other Workers require a degree?

USCIS defines Other Workers as jobs requiring less than 2 years of training or experience; no bachelor's degree is required. The worker must still meet the requirements in the job offer.

### Can my family come with me?

Yes. According to USCIS, once the I-140 is approved, the spouse and unmarried children under 21 can apply for green cards with or after the worker.

### What is PERM?

It's the Department of Labor's labor certification, confirming the employer recruited under the rules and found no qualified U.S. worker. Most EB-3 cases need PERM before the I-140.

### How long does EB-3 take?

It depends on PERM, I-140 and the Visa Bulletin line. PERM alone currently averages 336 days. See the wait-time article for details.

### Can I do EB-3 while in the U.S.?

You may be able to file Form I-485 to adjust status in the U.S. if eligible, depending on your current status. Ask a licensed immigration attorney about your case.

### How many EB-3 Other Workers visas are there each year?

Up to 10,000 a year for Other Workers, minus a NACARA reduction (about 150 in fiscal year 2027).
