The U.S. citizenship test is not hard for well-prepared applicants: according to the latest figures USCIS has published, **95.7%** of applicants passed in fiscal year 2022 (including retests), and more than 88% passed on the first try. Still, the 2025 civics test (for applications filed on or after October 20, 2025) has a larger question bank and asks more questions than the old test, and USCIS has not yet published a pass rate for the new test. Beyond the 128 civics questions, the part that needs the most preparation is understanding the English questions about your own Form N-400.

## What is the U.S. citizenship test pass rate?

According to the USCIS [Naturalization Test Performance](https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship-resource-center/naturalization-related-data-and-statistics/naturalization-test-performance) page (updated October 5, 2023, checked October 15, 2026), figures for fiscal year 2022:

| Measure (fiscal year 2022) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Overall pass rate (first try + retest) | 95.7% |
| Passed at the first interview | More than 88% |
| Passed on the retest | About 7% |
| Civics, first try, all applicants | 92.4% |
| English speaking, first try | 94% |
| English comprehension, first try | 93% |
| English writing, first try | 94% |
| English reading, first try | 97% |

First-try civics pass rates for applicants exempt from English because of age (fiscal year 2022): 88.7% for 50/20, 85.9% for 55/15 and 91.2% for 65/20.

**Important:** these figures predate the 2025 test. The USCIS page has no figures yet for the new 20-question test. Treat them as a general picture, not a prediction for your test.

## How is the 2025 test different from the old one?

According to [The Naturalization Interview and Test](https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship/learn-about-citizenship/the-naturalization-interview-and-test) and the [USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-e-chapter-2) (checked October 15, 2026):

| | 2008 test | 2025 test |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to applications filed | Before Oct 20, 2025 | On or after Oct 20, 2025 |
| Question bank | 100 questions | 128 questions |
| Questions asked | Up to 10 | Up to 20 |
| Needed to pass | 6 | 12 |
| Stops early | 6 correct or 5 wrong | 12 correct or 9 wrong |

The share you need right is still 60%. The main differences are that you **have more questions to learn** (128 instead of 100) and **must stay focused longer** (up to 20 questions). The Policy Manual says the questions are chosen at random by the USCIS system, so there are no "favorite" questions to bank on.

The full list: [The 128 Civics Questions (2025 Test) and How to Study Them](/en/blog/128-civics-questions-2025).

## Which part of the interview do people fail most?

USCIS does not publish reasons for failing by question. Based on how the test is structured and the official rules, these are the parts to watch:

### 1. Understanding questions about your Form N-400

There is no separate speaking test. According to USCIS, your speaking is assessed while the officer asks about your N-400 and background. The Policy Manual says you pass if you can generally understand and respond meaningfully to questions relevant to your case. In the 2022 figures above, "English comprehension" had a first-try pass rate of 93%, the lowest of the English parts.

The questions about your application use words that rarely come up in daily life, especially in the Part 9 Yes/No questions ("ever", "arrested", "overthrow", "totalitarian"…). [N-400 Yes/No Questions: What Each One Asks](/en/blog/n-400-yes-no-questions) explains each group of questions in plain language.

### 2. Writing: abbreviations and capitalization

You need to write 1 of up to 3 dictated sentences correctly. Under the USCIS Policy Manual, **abbreviating a word** in the dictated sentence counts as a fail. When you practice, write every word in full (for example "United States", not "U.S.", if the sentence says "United States"), and capitalize names, holidays and months.

### 3. Civics answers that change

Some answers change over time or depend on where you live: the President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, Chief Justice, your senators and representative, your governor and state capital. USCIS requires the person in office **on the day of your interview**. Memorizing names from old materials is a common mistake. The current list is at [uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates](https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates).

### 4. "Name two", "name three", "name five" questions

Some questions ask for several answers, for example question 81, "Name five of the 13 original states," or question 126, "Name three national U.S. holidays." Practice giving the full number the question asks for.

### 5. Nerves and not recognizing the question

Many people know the answer but don't recognize the question when it is spoken. The fix is to **practice listening and answering out loud**, not just reading silently.

## What happens if I fail?

According to USCIS, you get **two chances** to pass the English and civics tests. If you fail a part, you are rescheduled to retake it 60–90 days after your first interview, and you retake only the part you failed. If you fail a second time, your application is denied. Under the Policy Manual, missing the retest without a reason USCIS accepts also counts as a failed attempt.

## Who gets an easier test?

- **65/20** (age 65 or older, green card for 20 years or more): exempt from English, civics in the language of your choice, only 20 questions to study, 6 of 10 correct to pass.
- **50/20 and 55/15:** exempt from English; you still take the full civics test, in the language of your choice.
- **Form N-648:** an exception from English and/or civics because of a medical disability.

Details: [The Citizenship Test for Applicants 65 and Older](/en/blog/citizenship-test-over-65).

## How do I pass on the first try?

1. **Know which test you will take** (2008 or 2025) from your filing date, and check whether you qualify for an English exemption.
2. **Study civics a little every day**, reviewing the questions you miss more often than the ones you know.
3. **Practice writing short sentences**, every word in full, with correct capitals.
4. **Reread your own Form N-400** and make sure you understand every question in it.
5. **Take full mock interviews** covering the N-400 questions, reading, writing and 20 civics questions, so the length of the test feels normal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the U.S. citizenship test hard?

Not very, for people who prepare well: according to USCIS, 95.7% of applicants in fiscal year 2022 passed (including retests). Those figures predate the 2025 test, so they do not yet reflect the new 20-question test.

### What is the pass rate for the 2025 civics test?

As of October 15, 2026, the USCIS naturalization test data page has no separate pass rate for the 2025 test. The latest figures on the page are for fiscal year 2022.

### Is the 2025 test harder than the old one?

The 2025 test has 128 questions (instead of 100) and asks up to 20 (instead of 10), with 12 correct needed. The share you need right is still 60%, but there are more questions to learn.

### Which part do people fail most often?

USCIS does not publish reasons for failing by question. In the 2022 figures, English comprehension had the lowest first-try pass rate of the English parts (93%). Civics had a first-try pass rate of 92.4%.

### Do abbreviations count as wrong on the writing test?

Yes. Under the USCIS Policy Manual, abbreviating a word in the dictated sentence counts as a fail.

### Can I retake the citizenship test if I fail?

Yes. You get two chances. You retake the part you failed 60–90 days after your first interview; if you fail again, your application is denied.

### How long should I study?

It depends on the person. The most reliable approach is to study a little every day, review the questions you miss and take mock tests until you pass comfortably and consistently.
