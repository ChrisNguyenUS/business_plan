Sending money home to parents or siblings, or receiving money from family in Vietnam, is routine. U.S. law has two rules few people know about. The **sender** may have to file **Form 709** (gift tax return) if they send a lot. The **recipient** may have to file **Form 3520** if they receive a very large amount from a foreign person. Most families **owe no tax**, but the **reporting** can still be mandatory.

This article covers **U.S. tax** only. Vietnam has its own rules on receiving foreign currency, personal income tax and bank procedures; ask your bank and the Vietnamese tax authority about those.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **The sender** is responsible for gift tax, not the recipient. Money you send is **not tax-deductible**.
> - 2026 gift tax exclusion: **$19,000 per recipient**. Above that you usually **file Form 709**, but you rarely owe tax because of the **$15,000,000** lifetime exemption.
> - **Recipient** in the U.S.: gifts from a foreign person are **not income**, but if gifts from **one foreign person total more than $100,000 in a year**, you must file **Form 3520**.
> - Money from **selling your own property** in Vietnam is **not a gift**. The gain may be taxable in the U.S.
> - Parents in Vietnam are **not your dependents**, even if you support them.

## Sending money to Vietnam

### Is it taxed? Is it deductible?

Supporting family **isn't deductible** from your taxable income. A dependent must be a **U.S. citizen, resident alien, U.S. national, or a resident of Canada or Mexico** (IRS). So parents or children in Vietnam who aren't in those groups are **not** your dependents.

### Gift tax: the sender pays, not the recipient

The IRS states that **the donor is generally responsible for paying gift tax**. If you are a U.S. citizen or resident and give money to someone in Vietnam, this rule still applies.

- **2026 annual exclusion:** $19,000 **per recipient**. Send your mother $15,000 and your brother $15,000 in a year: each is under the exclusion, so there is no gift tax.
- **Spouses:** if you give together, each spouse can use their own exclusion, for a total of $38,000 per recipient.
- **Above the exclusion:** you usually **file Form 709** (the gift tax return) for that year. The excess isn't taxed right away; it **counts against your lifetime exemption of $15,000,000** (2026). Most people never reach it.
- **A spouse who isn't a U.S. citizen** has separate exclusion rules. Ask a preparer.

*Example:* in 2026 you send your mother in Vietnam $30,000 to repair her house. The amount over the exclusion is $11,000, so you file Form 709. No tax is due, but the $11,000 is recorded against your lifetime exemption.

## Receiving money from Vietnam

### Gifts from family: not income, but may need to be reported

According to the IRS, **a gift from a foreign person isn't included in the recipient's taxable income** (the IRS defines such a gift as an amount the recipient "excludes from gross income"). But if the gift is **large enough**, you must **report it** on **Form 3520**:

| The giver | When Form 3520 is required (2026) |
|---|---|
| **A nonresident alien or a foreign estate** | Gifts **total more than $100,000** in the year. Once over, you list **each gift over $5,000** separately |
| **A foreign corporation or partnership** | Total is over **$20,573** (2026) |

- **Deadline:** the 15th day of the 4th month after your tax year ends, so **April 15** for calendar-year filers. Extensions may apply.
- **Penalty for not filing:** **5% of the gift's value per month**, up to **25%**, unless there is reasonable cause.
- **Gifts from several relatives:** the Form 3520 instructions have a rule to **aggregate** gifts from foreign persons who are related to each other. Parents who send money separately may be combined. Ask a preparer if your total is near the line.
- **An inheritance (bequest) from a foreign estate** uses the same $100,000 threshold.

### Proceeds from selling property in Vietnam

**Whoever sells is the one taxed.**

- **Property in your name:** if you are a U.S. citizen or resident, your worldwide income is taxed in the U.S. Gain on selling property in Vietnam may need to be reported (measured in U.S. dollars, which may involve exchange rates at purchase and sale). If you **already paid tax in Vietnam** on it, you may be able to claim a **foreign tax credit (Form 1116)**.
- **Property in your parents' name, sold by them, with money sent to you:** that's a **gift** from a foreign person. If the total goes over $100,000 in a year, you file **Form 3520**.

### Moving your own money from Vietnam to the U.S.

Moving **your own money** from a Vietnamese account to a U.S. account is **not income** in itself. But the **interest** it earned is income you must report, and the account in Vietnam may have to be reported on the **FBAR/Form 8938**. See [FBAR and Form 8938 for Vietnam Bank Accounts](/en/blog/fbar-form-8938-vietnam-bank-accounts). A U.S. bank may ask about the source of large transfers, so keep documentation.

## The U.S. and Vietnam have no income tax treaty

According to the U.S. State Department's [2025 Investment Climate Statement for Vietnam](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/vietnam), the two countries have **no bilateral income tax treaty**. Avoiding double taxation therefore relies mainly on the **foreign tax credit (Form 1116)** on your U.S. return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is sending money to my parents in Vietnam taxable?

The recipient isn't taxed on it in the U.S. The sender is responsible for gift tax, but only if the gift is over the exclusion ($19,000 per recipient in 2026) and over the lifetime exemption, which is rare. You may still have to file Form 709 when you go over $19,000.

### I sent my mother $20,000 this year. Do I have to report it?

That's $1,000 over the $19,000 exclusion. You usually file Form 709 for that year, but you owe no tax because the excess counts against your lifetime exemption.

### My parents sent me $50,000. Do I have to report it?

A gift from a foreign person isn't income. Form 3520 is required only when gifts from one foreign person total more than $100,000 in the year. If both parents send money, ask a preparer whether the amounts are combined.

### When is Form 3520 due, and what's the penalty?

Usually April 15 for calendar-year filers. The penalty is 5% of the gift's value per month, up to 25%, unless there is reasonable cause.

### I sold a house in Vietnam in my name. Do I report it in the U.S.?

If you are a U.S. citizen or resident, possibly. The gain may be subject to U.S. tax, and you may get a credit if you paid tax in Vietnam. Keep the purchase and sale contracts.

### Are my parents in Vietnam my dependents?

No, unless they are U.S. citizens, U.S. residents, or residents of Canada or Mexico. Sending them money to support them doesn't change that.
