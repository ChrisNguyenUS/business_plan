Medicare is the federal health insurance program for people 65 and older, and for some younger people with disabilities. Every year from **October 15 to December 7**, people who already have Medicare can change plans for the next year. Changes you make now start on **January 1, 2027**.

If your parents immigrated to join you and are turning 65 or already past it, this guide covers whether they qualify, when to sign up, and what to review before December 7.

> **Quick summary**
> - **Deadline to change plans:** December 7, 2026. The plan must receive your request by then.
> - **First sign-up:** a 7-month window around your 65th birthday (3 months before, the birthday month, 3 months after).
> - **Green card holders:** usually need 5 years of continuous U.S. residence, or 10 years of Medicare-taxed work (yours or your spouse's).
> - **Drugs in 2027:** out-of-pocket drug costs capped at $2,400 a year; plan deductible no higher than $700.
> - **Free help in Texas:** HICAP, 1-800-252-9240.

## What are the parts of Medicare?

| Part | What it pays for |
|---|---|
| Part A | Hospital stays, short-term skilled nursing care, hospice |
| Part B | Doctor visits, lab tests, outpatient care, medical equipment |
| Part C (Medicare Advantage) | A private plan that bundles A and B, usually with drug coverage |
| Part D | Prescription drugs |

You get Medicare in one of two ways:

- **Original Medicare (A + B):** you can see any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare. Add a Part D plan for drugs. Many people also buy **Medigap**, a supplement that pays part of what you would otherwise owe.
- **Medicare Advantage (Part C):** one private plan replaces A and B. These plans usually have a provider network and may include dental, vision and hearing. In exchange, you usually need to stay in the network.

Medigap only works with Original Medicare. If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you cannot use a Medigap policy.

## Can green card holders get Medicare?

Yes, if they meet the age and residency rules. According to [KFF](https://www.kff.org/faqs/medicare-open-enrollment-faqs/can-immigrants-enroll-in-medicare/), there are two main paths:

- **Premium-free Part A:** you or your spouse worked and paid Medicare taxes for at least 40 quarters (about 10 years). On this path, how long you have lived in the U.S. does not matter.
- **Buying Part A and Part B:** without 40 quarters, a permanent resident 65 or older can still enroll after **5 years of continuous U.S. residence**, paying a monthly premium for Part A.

Many parents who immigrate later in life have not worked 10 years here, so they wait until they reach 5 years as permanent residents. While they wait, see our guide to [Marketplace health insurance](/en/blog/marketplace-open-enrollment-health-insurance) for other options.

### A 2025 law narrowed which immigrants can have Medicare

A federal law signed in July 2025 limits Medicare to U.S. citizens and nationals, permanent residents (green card holders), Cuban/Haitian entrants, and people living in the U.S. under the Compacts of Free Association. KFF notes that refugees, asylees and people with TPS are no longer eligible. If you have Medicare now and are not in an eligible group, your coverage will end **no later than January 4, 2027**.

If someone in your family is a refugee or asylee on Medicare, call Social Security or HICAP now to ask about next steps. Refugees can usually apply for a green card after one year in the U.S., so also check their current immigration status.

## When do you sign up for the first time?

[Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/sign-up/when-does-medicare-coverage-start) lists these windows:

| Window | Timing | When coverage starts |
|---|---|---|
| Initial Enrollment Period | 7 months: 3 months before your 65th birthday month, that month, and 3 months after | Sign up before your birthday month and Part B starts that month; sign up later and it starts the next month |
| General Enrollment Period | January 1 to March 31 each year | The month after you sign up; a late penalty may apply |
| Special Enrollment Period | After events such as losing job-based coverage | Depends on the situation |

People already getting Social Security benefits are usually enrolled in Parts A and B automatically. Everyone else signs up through Social Security. If you miss your first window without a qualifying reason, you may pay a **late enrollment penalty added to your Part B premium** for as long as you have Part B.

### Medigap: the 6 months that matter most

According to [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/ready-to-buy/when), you get **one 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period**, starting the first month you have Part B and are 65 or older. During those 6 months, an insurer cannot turn you down for pre-existing conditions. After that, you may be denied or charged more.

## October 15 to December 7: what can you change?

According to [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/get-more-coverage/joining-a-plan), during this window you can:

- Move from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage, or back.
- Switch from one Medicare Advantage plan to another.
- Join, drop or switch a Part D drug plan.

Changes take effect January 1. The plan must **receive** your request by December 7, so do not wait until the last day.

If you are in Medicare Advantage, you get one more window from **January 1 to March 31**: switch to another Advantage plan, or go back to Original Medicare and join a Part D plan. Those changes start the first day of the next month.

### Before December 7, review these 5 things

1. **Your Annual Notice of Change.** Your plan mails this in the fall. It lists next year's changes to costs, drugs and doctors.
2. **Your medications.** Check that each drug is on the plan's 2027 drug list (formulary) and what it will cost.
3. **Doctors and pharmacies.** Confirm your doctors stay in network and whether your pharmacy is a "preferred" one.
4. **Total cost.** Add up premiums, deductibles and copays. A $0 premium is only one piece.
5. **Language.** Advantage plans have their own networks. Ask whether there are doctors who speak your parents' language.

You can compare plans for free on [Medicare Plan Finder](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/).

## 2027 costs: what we know

- **Drugs (Part D):** starting in 2027, out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at **$2,400 a year** (up from $2,100 in 2026). No drug plan can have a deductible above **$700** (up from $615). Source: [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/drug-coverage-part-d/costs-for-medicare-drug-coverage/costs-in-the-coverage-gap).
- **Part B premium:** $202.90 a month in 2026. CMS usually announces the next year's amount in mid-November; as of November 10, 2026, the 2027 figure was not yet out.

People with limited income may qualify for **Extra Help** with drug costs and **Medicare Savings Programs** that help pay the Part B premium. Ask HICAP or Social Security how to apply.

## Watch out for scams

Open Enrollment season brings a lot of unwanted calls. Keep in mind:

- Medicare does not call to sell you a plan or to "issue a new card."
- Never give your Medicare number, Social Security number or bank account to someone who called you.
- Anyone selling insurance in Texas needs a license. Look them up with the [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) or call 1-800-252-3439.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does Medicare Open Enrollment end this year?

December 7, 2026. Your plan must receive your request by the end of that day. Changes take effect January 1, 2027.

### My parents have had green cards for 3 years and are 66. Can they buy Medicare?

Usually not yet, unless they worked 40 quarters in the U.S. Permanent residents need 5 years of continuous residence to buy Part A and Part B. If their spouse has 40 quarters, they may get premium-free Part A based on the spouse's work record.

### What happens to refugees who have Medicare now?

The July 2025 law removed refugees, asylees and people with TPS from Medicare eligibility. According to KFF, their coverage ends no later than January 4, 2027. Anyone who already has a green card stays eligible. Call Social Security or HICAP about a specific case.

### How is Medicare Advantage different from Medigap?

Medicare Advantage is a private plan that replaces Parts A and B and usually has a provider network. Medigap is a supplement that works with Original Medicare and pays part of your share of costs. You cannot have both at once.

### I missed December 7. Can I still change plans?

If you are in Medicare Advantage, you can change between January 1 and March 31. If you have Original Medicare, you usually wait for the next window unless a special event applies, such as moving out of your plan's service area.

### What is the most I will pay for drugs in 2027?

According to Medicare.gov, out-of-pocket Part D drug costs are capped at $2,400 for 2027. Drug plan deductibles cannot exceed $700.

### Who can help me compare plans for free?

In Texas, call HICAP at 1-800-252-9240. You can also call 1-800-MEDICARE and ask for an interpreter.
