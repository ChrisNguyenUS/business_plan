A birth certificate is the core document proving that you or your child was **born in the U.S.** and is a U.S. citizen. You need a certified copy to apply for a passport, for some government paperwork and for many other procedures. One thing few people know: **the federal government doesn't issue birth certificates**. You order one from the vital records office of the **state or territory where the birth took place**.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Order from the **vital records office of the state where you were born**, no matter where you live now.
> - How to order (online, by mail or in person), fees and processing times **vary by state**.
> - You'll need to give: **full name, parents' names (including the mother's maiden name), date and place of birth, and the hospital** if known.
> - For official procedures you need a **certified copy**, not a photocopy.
> - A U.S. citizen **born abroad** may have a **Consular Report of Birth Abroad (CRBA)** instead of a U.S. birth certificate.

## Where to order a certified copy

According to the CDC's National Center for Health Statistics, to get a copy of a birth certificate you must **write to or visit the vital records office in the state or area where the event occurred**. The CDC's [Where to Write for Vital Records](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/w2w/index.htm) page lists each state with contact details. USA.gov also tells you to contact the birth state's vital records office to learn how to order online, by mail or in person, and what it costs.

The CDC page is clear: **the federal government does not distribute birth certificates** or vital records files. If you were born in a U.S. territory, you order from that territory's vital records office.

Watch out for websites that add "service" fees far above what the government office charges. Start from your state's website or the CDC list.

## What information to provide

The CDC advises having ready:

- The full name of the person on the certificate.
- The parents' names, **including the mother's maiden name**.
- Date of birth and place of birth (city and county).
- The hospital name, if known.

You'll also need to prove you're eligible to request the copy, usually with a photo ID. Each state sets who may order (the person, a parent, a child, a spouse and so on). If you have no ID at all, USA.gov says many states have another way to verify your identity, such as a sworn statement.

## Certified copy or photocopy?

For a passport, school enrollment or other government procedures, you usually need a **certified copy** issued by the vital records office, with the office's seal or signature. A photocopy is usually not accepted. Ask the agency receiving your papers which type it needs.

## A newborn

The hospital typically collects information to **register the birth with the state** and, if you agree, applies for your baby's SSN at the same time. After that you order certified copies from the state vital records office. See [Having a Baby in the U.S.](/en/blog/having-a-baby-in-the-us-paperwork).

## Born abroad

If you are a U.S. citizen **born abroad**, you may have a **Consular Report of Birth Abroad (CRBA)** issued by a U.S. embassy or consulate. USA.gov says it serves the same purpose as a U.S. birth certificate. A Vietnamese birth certificate cannot replace a U.S. birth certificate when you are proving citizenship.

## What is a birth certificate used for?

- Applying for a **U.S. passport**. See [Getting Your First U.S. Passport](/en/blog/first-us-passport-fees-documents).
- Proving your name and date of birth for a [REAL ID](/en/blog/real-id-driver-license-new-to-us).
- School enrollment, insurance and other administrative procedures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I order a U.S. birth certificate?

From the vital records office of the state or territory where you were born. The federal government doesn't issue birth certificates.

### I live in a different state now. What do I do?

You still order from your birth state. Many states take online or mail orders, but the process and fees vary.

### What information do I need to provide?

Your full name, your parents' names including your mother's maiden name, date and place of birth, the hospital if you know it, plus proof that you're eligible to order.

### Can I use a photocopy?

For a passport or government procedures, you usually need a certified copy issued by the vital records office. Ask the receiving agency which type it needs.

### I was born in Vietnam but I'm a U.S. citizen. What proves it?

You may have a Consular Report of Birth Abroad issued by a U.S. embassy or consulate, or a certificate of citizenship. A Vietnamese birth certificate can't replace those.

### I lost all my ID. How do I order a birth certificate?

Ask your birth state's vital records office. Many states have another way to verify identity, for example a sworn statement.
