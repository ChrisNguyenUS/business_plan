Form N-400 (Application for Naturalization) is the form a green card holder files with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) to become a U.S. citizen. Applicants usually need to have had a green card for at least 5 years (or 3 years if married to and living with a U.S. citizen) and meet the requirements for residence, good moral character, English and civics. According to the [USCIS N-400 page](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400) (checked October 15, 2026), the fee is **$710 online** and **$760 on paper**, and you can ask for a reduced fee or a waiver.

This article explains who can file, the fees, the parts of the form and what happens after you file. The full journey from filing to the oath ceremony is in [U.S. Citizenship Test 2026: Every Step from Form N-400 to the Oath Ceremony](/en/blog/us-citizenship-test-guide).

## Who can file Form N-400?

According to USCIS, there are several ways to qualify. The two most common:

### Green card holder for 5 years

According to [I Am a Lawful Permanent Resident of 5 Years](https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship/learn-about-citizenship/citizenship-and-naturalization/i-am-a-lawful-permanent-resident-of-5-years) (updated March 16, 2026), you need to:

- Be at least 18 years old when you file.
- Have been a permanent resident (green card holder) for at least 5 years.
- Have **continuous residence** in the U.S. for at least 5 years immediately before filing.
- Have **physical presence** in the U.S. for at least **30 months** of those 5 years.
- Have lived for at least **3 months** in the state or USCIS district where you file.
- Show **good moral character** for the 5 years before filing (and until the oath).
- Read, write and speak basic English and know the basics of U.S. history and government.
- Be attached to the principles of the U.S. Constitution.

### Married to a U.S. citizen (3 years)

According to [I Am Married to a U.S. Citizen](https://www.uscis.gov/citizenship/learn-about-citizenship/citizenship-and-naturalization/i-am-married-to-a-us-citizen), you need a green card for at least 3 years, **living in marital union** with your U.S. citizen spouse during those 3 years, 3 years of continuous residence, at least **18 months** of physical presence in those 3 years, 3 months in the state or district where you file, plus the moral character, English, civics and Constitution requirements.

There are other bases too, such as service in the U.S. military. The N-400 instructions list them under Part 1.

### How is "continuous residence" different from "physical presence"?

- **Physical presence** is the total number of days you were actually inside the United States. Form N-400 asks about your trips outside the U.S. to work this out.
- **Continuous residence** means keeping the U.S. as your home without a break. Long trips abroad can affect it.

How these are counted in a particular case, especially after long periods outside the U.S., is a question for a licensed immigration attorney.

### Can I file early?

According to the [Form N-400 instructions (edition 01/20/25)](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/n-400instr.pdf), applicants on the 5-year or 3-year basis can generally file **up to 90 days** before they meet the continuous residence requirement, as long as they meet every other requirement. USCIS has an Early Filing Calculator to work out the date.

## How much is the Form N-400 filing fee?

According to [uscis.gov/n-400](https://www.uscis.gov/n-400) (checked October 15, 2026):

| Case | Fee | How to file |
|---|---|---|
| Full fee, online | $710 | Online |
| Full fee, paper | $760 | Paper |
| Reduced fee | $380 | Paper only |
| Fee waiver (Form I-912) | $0 | Paper only |

### Who qualifies for a reduced fee or a fee waiver?

According to the [USCIS Poverty Guidelines page](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-fees/poverty-guidelines) (in effect from January 13, 2026, checked October 15, 2026), for the 48 contiguous states and D.C.:

| Household size | Fee waiver (≤150% of poverty guidelines) | N-400 reduced fee (≤400% of poverty guidelines) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $23,940 | $63,840 |
| 4 | $49,500 | $132,000 |

Alaska and Hawaii have separate tables on the same page.

- **Reduced fee of $380:** household income at or below 400% of the federal poverty guidelines, with supporting documents, requested in Part 10 of Form N-400.
- **Fee waiver with Form I-912:** under the Form I-912 instructions, based on one of three grounds: receiving a means-tested benefit (such as Medicaid or SNAP), household income at or below 150% of the poverty guidelines, or financial hardship. Form N-400 is on the list of forms eligible for a waiver on the [I-912 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-912).

Fees can change. Check uscis.gov/n-400 and the [G-1055 Fee Schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) on the day you file.

## What are the parts of Form N-400?

The 01/20/25 edition of Form N-400 has 16 parts (per the table of contents in the [instructions](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/n-400instr.pdf)):

| Part | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1 | Information about your eligibility (the basis you are filing under) |
| 2 | Personal information: name, date of birth, green card date, nationality, disability exception request (item 11) |
| 3 | Biographic information: ethnicity, race, height, weight, eye and hair color |
| 4 | Where you have lived in the last 5 (or 3) years |
| 5 | Marital status and prior marriages |
| 6 | Information about your children |
| 7 | Employment and schools in the last 5 years |
| 8 | Time outside the United States (trips) |
| 9 | Additional information: the Yes/No questions |
| 10 | Request for a reduced fee |
| 11 | Applicant's contact information, certification and signature |
| 12 | Interpreter (if any) |
| 13 | Preparer (if someone other than the applicant) |
| 14 | Additional information (extra space and explanations) |
| 15 | Signature at the interview |
| 16 | Oath of Allegiance |

Part 9 is the part most people need to read most carefully. [N-400 Yes/No Questions: What Each One Asks](/en/blog/n-400-yes-no-questions) explains each group of questions.

A few points from the N-400 page and the instructions:

- USCIS rejects an application **without a signature**.
- Every page must be the same edition, with the edition date at the bottom.
- If you need more space, use Part 14.
- In Part 11 you certify, under penalty of perjury, that all the information is complete, true and correct.

## Should I file Form N-400 online or on paper?

According to USCIS, you can file online through a USCIS account or on paper. Filing online costs $50 less. However, **anyone requesting a reduced fee or a fee waiver must file on paper**.

## What happens after I file Form N-400?

1. **Biometrics:** according to uscis.gov/n-400, N-400 applicants generally must provide fingerprints unless exempt. USCIS mails an appointment notice if you need to visit an Application Support Center.
2. **Interview and tests:** the officer asks about your application and tests your English (speaking, reading and writing) and civics. Applicants who filed on or after October 20, 2025 take the 2025 test (up to 20 of 128 questions, 12 correct to pass). See [The 128 Civics Questions (2025 Test) and How to Study Them](/en/blog/128-civics-questions-2025).
3. **Result (N-652):** granted, continued or denied.
4. **Oath ceremony:** you become a U.S. citizen after you take the Oath of Allegiance.

Processing times depend on the office; see [USCIS Processing Times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/).

Older applicants may be exempt from the English test. See [The Citizenship Test for Applicants 65 and Older](/en/blog/citizenship-test-over-65).

## What should I gather before filling out the form?

Form N-400 asks about the last 5 (or 3) years in detail. Having this ready makes it faster and more accurate:

- Your green card (both sides) and A-Number.
- Your home addresses, with start and end dates, for 5 years.
- Your employers or schools, with dates, for 5 years.
- The departure and return dates of every trip outside the U.S. in 5 years.
- Current and prior marriages, and information about your children.
- If they apply: documents about overdue taxes, arrests or court cases (see the document checklist in the instructions).
- If you are asking for a reduced fee or a waiver: proof of income or of the benefit you receive.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Form N-400?

Form N-400 (Application for Naturalization) is the application for U.S. citizenship, filed with USCIS. Applicants are usually permanent residents (green card holders) who have met the residence requirements in the law.

### How long do I need a green card before filing Form N-400?

Usually 5 years, or 3 years if you are married to and living with a U.S. citizen during those 3 years. According to the N-400 instructions, you can file up to 90 days before you meet the continuous residence requirement.

### How much is the N-400 fee in 2026?

According to uscis.gov/n-400 (checked October 15, 2026): $710 online, $760 on paper, $380 with the reduced fee, and $0 with an approved Form I-912 fee waiver. Fees can change, so check again before you file.

### What income qualifies for the reduced N-400 fee?

Households with income at or below 400% of the federal poverty guidelines can request the $380 reduced fee. Under the USCIS table in effect from January 13, 2026, that is $63,840 for a 1-person household and $132,000 for a 4-person household in the 48 contiguous states.

### Can I request a fee waiver online?

No. According to uscis.gov/n-400, applicants requesting a fee waiver or a reduced fee must file Form N-400 on paper.

### How many parts does Form N-400 have?

The 01/20/25 edition has 16 parts, from your eligibility (Part 1) to the Oath of Allegiance (Part 16). Part 9 contains the Yes/No questions.

### Can I fill out Form N-400 myself?

Yes. USCIS provides the form and instructions free at uscis.gov/n-400. If someone else prepares it for you, that person fills in their information in Part 13.

### How long after filing Form N-400 is the interview?

It depends on the USCIS office. You can check current processing times on the USCIS Processing Times page.
