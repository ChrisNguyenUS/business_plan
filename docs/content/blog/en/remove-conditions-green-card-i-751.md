A 2-year green card is a **conditional permanent resident card**, usually issued to people who got their green card through marriage when the marriage was less than 2 years old on the day the card was granted. To get a 10-year card, you file **Form I-751 (Petition to Remove Conditions on Residence)** in the **90 days before the card expires**. The fee under the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026) is **$700 online** and **$750 on paper**. A 2-year card **cannot be renewed**: if you don't file Form I-751, you lose your permanent resident status.

## What is a 2-year green card?

According to the USCIS [Conditional Permanent Residence](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/conditional-permanent-residence) page (checked October 19, 2026), a conditional permanent resident receives a green card valid for **2 years**. You have the same rights as other permanent residents: to live, work and travel. The difference is that you must ask to remove the conditions before the card expires.

USCIS is clear: you **cannot renew** a conditional green card with Form I-90. If your conditions are not removed, you **lose your permanent resident status and become removable** from the United States.

If you are preparing to sponsor your spouse, see also [Sponsoring Your Spouse for a Green Card](/en/blog/sponsor-spouse-green-card).

## When do I file Form I-751?

According to the [USCIS I-751 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-751) (updated June 16, 2026):

- **Joint filing with your spouse:** file during the **90-day period immediately before your card expires**. Filing earlier may get the petition rejected. USCIS has a [page to find the first day you can file](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/when-to-file-your-petition-to-remove-conditions).
- **Waiver of the joint filing requirement:** file on your own **at any time before your card expires**, if you qualify on one of these grounds:
  - a good-faith marriage that ended in divorce or annulment;
  - your spouse died;
  - you were battered or subjected to extreme cruelty;
  - removal would cause extreme hardship.
- **Children:** children who got conditional cards at the same time as a parent are usually included in the parent's Form I-751.

An example to show how the window works (not a calculation for your case): if your card expires on June 1, the joint filing window opens about 90 days earlier, in early March.

## How much is the Form I-751 fee?

According to the [G-1055 Fee Schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) (edition October 16, 2026, checked October 19, 2026):

| How you file | Form I-751 fee |
|---|---|
| Online | $700 |
| Paper | $750 |
| Waiver based on battery or extreme cruelty | $0 |

Fees can change, so check again on the day you file.

## What evidence shows the marriage is real?

According to USCIS [Removing Conditions on Permanent Residence Based on Marriage](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/conditional-permanent-residence/removing-conditions-on-permanent-residence-based-on-marriage), you submit evidence that the marriage is genuine (bona fide), not entered only to get a green card. Common examples from the Form I-751 instructions:

- Housing: a lease or deed in both names.
- Shared finances: joint bank accounts, credit cards, insurance listing each other.
- Birth certificates of children born to the marriage (if any).
- Joint tax returns.
- Sworn affidavits from people who know you both, with their contact information.

Send documents from across the full 2 years of your marriage.

## What happens after I file Form I-751?

1. **Receipt notice (Form I-797):** according to a [USCIS announcement](https://www.uscis.gov/archive/uscis-extends-green-card-validity-for-conditional-permanent-residents-with-a-pending-form-i-751-or), the Form I-751 receipt notice together with your (expired) 2-year card proves permanent resident status for **48 months** from the expiration date on the card. Use them for work and travel while you wait.
2. **Biometrics:** USCIS may schedule a biometrics appointment.
3. **Interview:** USCIS may call you for an interview or decide without one.
4. **Decision:** if approved, you receive a 10-year green card.

Current processing times are on the [USCIS Processing Times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) page (select Form I-751).

## Can I apply for citizenship with a 2-year card?

Your time as a conditional resident **counts** toward the residence required for naturalization. If you are married to and living with a U.S. citizen, you may be able to file Form N-400 after 3 years as a permanent resident. Details are in [What Is Form N-400?](/en/blog/what-is-form-n-400). If your Form I-751 is still pending when you file Form N-400, USCIS generally reviews both; ask a licensed immigration attorney about your own situation.

When you prepare for the citizenship test, you can practice free with [N400 Ready](/en/n400ready).

## Can I travel abroad with a 2-year card?

Yes. A valid card, or an expired card with the Form I-751 receipt notice, lets you return to the U.S. after temporary travel. The rules on time spent abroad are the same as for every permanent resident; see [How Long Can a Green Card Holder Stay Outside the U.S.?](/en/blog/green-card-travel-how-long-abroad). Don't miss the Form I-751 deadline because you are traveling.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a 2-year green card?

A conditional permanent resident card valid for 2 years, usually issued when a marriage was less than 2 years old on the day the green card was granted. To get a 10-year card, you file Form I-751.

### Can a 2-year green card be renewed?

No. USCIS says a conditional card cannot be renewed. You file Form I-751 to remove the conditions.

### When do I file Form I-751?

File jointly with your spouse in the 90 days immediately before your card expires. If you request a waiver of joint filing, you can file any time before the card expires.

### How much is the Form I-751 fee?

Under the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026): $700 online, $750 on paper. Fees can change, so check again before you file.

### What happens if I don't file Form I-751?

According to USCIS, if your conditions are not removed, you lose your permanent resident status and become removable from the United States.

### My card expired while Form I-751 is pending. What do I use?

According to USCIS, the Form I-751 receipt notice together with your expired card proves permanent resident status for 48 months from the expiration date on the card.

### Can I still get a 10-year card after a divorce?

Possibly, by filing Form I-751 with a waiver of the joint filing requirement if the marriage was entered in good faith. Talk to a licensed immigration attorney about this case.
