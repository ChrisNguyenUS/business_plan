Form 1099-NEC reports to the IRS what your business paid someone who is not your employee, such as a nail technician working as an independent contractor. For payments made in 2026, you file a 1099-NEC for each worker you paid **$2,000** or more. Both the worker's copy and the IRS copy are due **February 1, 2027**.

The $2,000 threshold is new. Last year it was $600, and many salon owners still have the old number in mind. This guide covers what changed, what to gather now, and the hardest question: is your technician an employee or a contractor?

> **Quick summary**
> - **Threshold:** $2,000 per worker for the year, for payments made on or after January 1, 2026. Payments made in 2025 still used $600.
> - **Deadline:** January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday, so the deadline is Monday, **February 1, 2027**, for both the worker's copy and the IRS copy.
> - **E-filing:** required if you file 10 or more information returns in the year (W-2s count toward the 10).
> - **First step:** collect a Form W-9 from each worker for their correct name and SSN, ITIN or EIN.
> - **Calling someone a "1099 worker" does not make them a contractor.** The IRS and Texas look at how you control the work.

## When does the $2,000 threshold start?

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed in July 2025, raised the reporting threshold for Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC from $600 to $2,000 for payments made after December 31, 2025. The [IRS instructions for Form 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec) say the amount may be adjusted for inflation starting in 2027.

| Payments made in | 1099-NEC threshold | Filing deadline |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $600 | February 2, 2026 (passed) |
| 2026 | $2,000 | February 1, 2027 |
| 2027 and later | $2,000, may be adjusted for inflation | Around January 31 of the next year |

If a technician worked a few weeks and earned $1,500 from your salon in 2026, you are not required to file a 1099-NEC for them. The technician still has to report that income on their own tax return, with or without a 1099.

## When do you have to file a 1099-NEC?

According to the IRS, you file when all four of these are true:

1. The person is **not your employee**.
2. You paid them **for services in your business**, not for something personal.
3. You paid an **individual, partnership or estate**. Payments to corporations are generally not reported (legal fees are an exception).
4. You paid them **$2,000 or more** in the year.

Besides technicians, salons often pay contract plumbers, electricians, web designers and cleaners. The same rules apply to them.

Payments you make by credit card or through a payment app's business features (such as PayPal or Venmo for business) do **not** go on Form 1099-NEC. The payment processor reports those on Form 1099-K.

## Start now: W-9s and records

Do not wait until late January to ask for Social Security numbers. Do these three things now:

- **Get a W-9 from each worker.** [Form W-9](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9) gives you their name and taxpayer ID (SSN, ITIN or EIN). The name and number must match IRS records.
- **Total what you paid each person.** Track cash, checks and Zelle month by month, and keep it with your salon's books.
- **If a worker will not give you a taxpayer ID:** the IRS requires you to withhold 24% (backup withholding) once their payments reach the reporting threshold, and send it to the IRS. You report that amount in box 4 of the 1099-NEC.

## How do you file?

- **Worker's copy (Copy B):** give it on paper, or electronically if the worker agrees, by February 1, 2027.
- **IRS copy (Copy A):** if you file 10 or more information returns in the year, counting W-2s and all 1099s together, you must e-file. With fewer than 10, you may file on paper with Form 1096.
- **Free e-filing:** the IRS runs the [IRIS portal](https://www.irs.gov/filing/e-file-information-returns-with-iris). You need a Transmitter Control Code (TCC) first, and approval takes time, so apply early.
- **Texas:** Texas has no state income tax, so Texas salons file only with the IRS.

Late or incorrect forms carry a penalty per form, and the penalty grows the later you file. If you find a mistake after filing, file a corrected form as soon as you can.

## Is your technician an employee or a contractor?

This is where salons run into the most trouble. A "1099" label or a signed "independent contractor" agreement does not settle it. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee) looks at three groups of evidence:

| Group | What the IRS asks |
|---|---|
| Behavioral | Does the salon have the right to direct what the worker does and how: hours, methods, which clients |
| Financial | Who sets prices, who pays for tools and supplies, can the worker make or lose money |
| Relationship | Is there a contract or benefits, is the work ongoing, is it a core part of the salon's business |

The [Texas Workforce Commission](https://www.twc.texas.gov/programs/unemployment-tax/classifying-employees-independent-contractors) uses a similar 20-factor guide for state unemployment tax, and states plainly that neither a contract nor a 1099 turns an employee into a contractor. TWC looks at whether the salon has the **right** to direct the work, even if it never uses that right.

No single factor decides the case; you weigh the whole picture of how your salon runs. If you treat an employee as a contractor without a reasonable basis, the IRS can make the salon pay the employment taxes it should have withheld and paid.

If you are unsure, you can ask officially:

- **IRS Form SS-8:** either the salon or the worker can file it. The IRS says a determination may take at least six months.
- **TWC Form C-12:** asks Texas to decide a worker's status for state unemployment tax.

If you have several technicians, talk with a licensed tax professional before you decide.

## What the technician should know

A technician who gets a 1099-NEC pays their own income tax and self-employment tax (about 15.3% of net earnings), since the salon withholds nothing. Most need to make quarterly estimated payments. See our guide to [self-employment tax](/en/blog/llc-owner-taxes-self-employment-tax).

A technician paid under $2,000 who gets no 1099 still has to report that income.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do I have to pay a worker in 2026 before I file a 1099-NEC?

$2,000 or more for the year, per person. This replaces the old $600 threshold and applies to payments made on or after January 1, 2026.

### When is the 1099-NEC due for 2026 payments?

February 1, 2027, because January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. The deadline applies to both the worker's copy and the IRS copy.

### I pay technicians in cash. Do I still report it?

Yes. Cash, checks and bank transfers all count once the total reaches $2,000 for the year. Payments by credit card or through a payment app's business features do not go on Form 1099-NEC.

### What if a worker has no Social Security number?

They can use an ITIN. If a worker gives you no taxpayer ID, the IRS requires you to withhold 24% once payments reach the reporting threshold and send it to the IRS.

### Is a signed independent contractor agreement enough?

No. The IRS and the Texas Workforce Commission look at how much control the salon has over the work, who sets prices and who pays for tools. A contract or a 1099 does not turn an employee into a contractor.

### Do I file 1099-NEC forms with the State of Texas?

No. Texas has no state income tax, so Texas salons file only with the IRS. Salons in other states should check their own state's rules.

### Can I file 1099-NEC forms online for free?

Yes, through the IRS IRIS portal. You need a Transmitter Control Code first, so apply early enough to meet the deadline.
