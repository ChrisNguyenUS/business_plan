A small business that wants to be found on Google needs four things. It needs its own **domain name** and **email on that domain** (for example hello@yourcompany.com). It needs a **website** with clear information that reads well on a phone, and a verified **Google Business Profile** (your listing on Google Maps). A Google Business Profile is only for businesses that **customers can visit** or that **travel to customers**; virtual offices and P.O. boxes are not accepted.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Register the domain in your own or your company's name, with branded email.
> - Keep name, address and phone identical on your website and Google Business Profile.
> - Google Business Profile rejects virtual offices, P.O. boxes and keyword-stuffed names.

## 1. Domain name: choose once, keep for years

- Short, easy to say over the phone, close to the name on your sign.
- Prefer **.com**. Avoid hyphens and hard-to-read numbers.
- Register the domain **in your company's or your own name**, not your web designer's, so you keep it if you change designers.
- Turn on auto-renew so it never lapses.

## 2. Email on your domain

An address like name@yourcompany.com looks more professional than a personal Gmail address and matches your company name when customers or banks check. Set up at least:

- One shared address for customers (info@ or hello@).
- One address for the owner, used with banks and government agencies.

Add the anti-spoofing records (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) so your mail doesn't land in spam.

## 3. Website: what customers and Google need to see

According to [Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), useful, clear content written for people matters more than any technical trick. For a small business, a good site has:

- **Name, address and phone** exactly as on your Google Business Profile and your sign.
- **Hours**, service area, and how to book or get in touch.
- **One page per main service**, saying what you do and for whom.
- **Vietnamese and English** if your customers include both. Give each language its own URL so Google can tell them apart.
- **Fast on phones.** Most people looking for a nearby shop or service search on their phone.
- **Real photos** of your place and work, with descriptions (alt text).
- **A contact or booking form** that goes straight to your email.

## 4. Google Business Profile: follow the rules so it isn't suspended

A **Google Business Profile** is your free listing on Google Search and Maps. According to [Google Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):

**Who can have a profile:**
- Businesses with **a location customers can visit during stated hours**, or that **travel to customers** (home repair, cleaning, delivery).
- Individual practitioners (doctors, lawyers) can have their own profile if customers can reach them directly at that location.

**Address:**
- **No** P.O. boxes or mailboxes at a remote location.
- **No** virtual offices, meaning a rented mailing address where the business doesn't operate.
- A co-working space qualifies only with clear signage, customers received during business hours, and your own staff on site.
- Businesses that **travel to customers** should **hide their address** and list a service area.

**Business name:**
- Must **match the real-world name** on your sign, website and stationery.
- **Don't** add keywords, taglines, phone numbers, hours or service descriptions to the name. Extra information can get the profile **suspended**.
- Include "LLC" only if your real-world name (on signs or invoices) uses it.

After verification: pick accurate categories, fill in hours and photos, ask happy customers for reviews, and reply to reviews politely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a small business need a website if it has a Facebook page?

Facebook keeps you in touch with regulars, but a website and a Google Business Profile help new customers find you on Google. A website is also information you control, not tied to a social network.

### Can I use a virtual office address on Google Business Profile?

No. Under Google's guidelines, virtual offices and P.O. boxes aren't eligible.

### I work at customers' homes. Can I have a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Set it up as a service-area business, hide your address and list the areas you serve.

### Will adding keywords to my Google Maps name help me rank?

Don't. Google requires your real-world name, and adding keywords or extra information can get the profile suspended.

### Whose name should the domain be in?

Your company's or yours, not your web designer's, so you always keep control.

### Does my website need two languages?

If your customers include Vietnamese speakers and English speakers, yes. Give each language its own URL so Google shows the right version to each person.
