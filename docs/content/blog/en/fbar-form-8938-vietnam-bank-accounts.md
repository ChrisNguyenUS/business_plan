Many Vietnamese-Americans still keep a **savings account or bank account in Vietnam**: savings, money for parents, or proceeds from selling property. U.S. law treats these as **foreign financial accounts**, and you may have to report them to the U.S. government even if the account **earns no interest** or you **never withdrew** a dollar. There are two separate reports: the **FBAR** and **Form 8938 (FATCA)**.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The **FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)** is required if the **combined balance** of your foreign accounts **exceeds $10,000 at any time** during the year.
> - The FBAR is filed **electronically with FinCEN**, not with your tax return. It's due **April 15**, with an automatic extension to **October 15** (no request needed).
> - **Form 8938** has higher thresholds and is **filed with your tax return**. A single filer living in the U.S. must file if foreign assets exceed **$50,000** at year-end or **$75,000** at any time.
> - The two reports are **separate**. Filing one doesn't satisfy the other.
> - An account in your parents' name where **you have signature authority** may also need to be reported.

## Who has to file?

The IRS says the FBAR applies to a **"U.S. person"**, which includes **citizens, residents, and U.S. corporations, partnerships and LLCs**. If you have a green card or spend enough days in the U.S. to be a tax resident (see [Your First U.S. Tax Return](/en/blog/first-us-tax-return-new-immigrants)), you are in this group. An LLC of yours that has an account in Vietnam may also have to file.

## Which accounts count?

- According to the IRS, an account **at a financial institution located outside the U.S.** is a foreign financial account. Whether the account produced taxable income has **no effect**.
- Reporting applies when you have a **financial interest** in the account or **signature authority or other authority** over it.
- Common examples: a savings account in your name; a joint account with a sibling; an account in your parents' name where you can sign or withdraw.
- Real estate in your name in Vietnam is **not** an "account" for the FBAR. But **rent from it must still be reported** on your income tax return.

### How the $10,000 is counted

You **add up the highest balance of each account** (converted to U.S. dollars) and see whether the total **exceeds $10,000 at any time during the year**. The IRS describes the threshold as the **aggregate** value of all your foreign accounts.

*Example:* three accounts in Vietnam with peak balances of about $4,000, $3,500 and $3,000, for a total of $10,500. Each account is under $10,000, but the **total is over the threshold**, so you must file an FBAR.

Balances in Vietnamese dong must be **converted to dollars**. The U.S. Treasury publishes exchange rates for this; see its Treasury Reporting Rates of Exchange page.

## FBAR: deadline and how to file

| | FBAR |
|---|---|
| **Form** | FinCEN Form 114 |
| **Threshold** | Combined foreign account balances over **$10,000** at any time in the year |
| **Deadline** | **April 15** of the following year |
| **Extension** | **Automatic to October 15**, no request needed |
| **Where to file** | **Electronically** through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System (not with your tax return) |

For example, the FBAR for **2026** is due **April 15, 2027**, automatically extended to **October 15, 2027**.

**Spouses with joint accounts.** The IRS says you **don't need to file a separate FBAR** if **all** of your foreign accounts are **jointly owned with your spouse** and your spouse files for you with **FinCEN Form 114a** (an authorization). Your tax filing status doesn't affect this.

## Form 8938 (FATCA): higher thresholds, filed with your return

Form 8938 reports **specified foreign financial assets**. It is **filed with your tax return**, on the same deadline as the return (including extensions).

| Living in the U.S. | Over this at year-end | Over this at any time during the year |
|---|---|---|
| Single or married filing separately | $50,000 | $75,000 |
| Married filing jointly | $100,000 | $150,000 |

People **living outside the U.S.** have higher thresholds: $200,000 at year-end or $300,000 at any time (single), and $400,000 and $600,000 (married filing jointly).

## How the FBAR and Form 8938 differ

| | FBAR | Form 8938 |
|---|---|---|
| Agency | FinCEN (Treasury) | IRS |
| Filed with your return? | No | **Yes** |
| Threshold (living in the U.S.) | $10,000 combined, at any time | $50,000 at year-end or $75,000 at any time (single) |
| Deadline | April 15, automatic extension to October 15 | Same as your tax return |

You may have to file **both**. An account in Vietnam holding $60,000, for example, would generally be reported on the **FBAR** and, depending on your situation, on **Form 8938**. Filing one doesn't replace the other.

## What if you don't file?

- **Form 8938:** a penalty of up to **$10,000** for failing to disclose, plus **$10,000 for each 30 days** you keep not filing after the IRS sends a notice, up to **$60,000**. Criminal penalties may also apply.
- **FBAR:** there are civil penalties and possibly criminal penalties. Maximum civil penalties are **adjusted for inflation every year**; see 31 CFR 1010.821. Willful violations are penalized far more heavily than non-willful ones.

## Missed past years?

The IRS has **Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures** for individuals whose failure to report was **non-willful** (due to negligence, inadvertence or mistake). You must certify that the failure was **non-willful**, and the program is **not available** if the IRS has already started a civil exam of your returns. If you're worried, talk to a preparer who handles international cases **before** you file anything, especially if the amounts are large.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much money in a Vietnam account triggers the FBAR?

When the combined balance of your foreign accounts exceeds $10,000 at any time during the year. You add the accounts together; you don't test each account on its own.

### Do I report an account that earns no interest?

Yes. The IRS says whether the account produced taxable income has no effect on whether it must be reported.

### I only have signature authority on my parents' account in Vietnam. Do I report it?

Possibly. The reporting rule applies when you have signature or other authority over an account, not only when you own it. Count that account in your total.

### Who do I file the FBAR with, and when is it due?

File it electronically with FinCEN through the BSA E-Filing System, not with your tax return. It's due April 15, with an automatic extension to October 15 and no request needed.

### Do I have to file both the FBAR and Form 8938?

Possibly. The two reports have different thresholds and go to different agencies. If you exceed both thresholds, you file both.

### Do I report a house in Vietnam in my name on the FBAR?

Real estate in your name isn't a financial account, so it isn't an FBAR item. But rent must be reported on your income tax return, and the proceeds from selling it may be subject to U.S. capital gains tax.

### I forgot to file the FBAR for several years. What now?

If you didn't willfully skip it, you may qualify for the IRS Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures. Talk to a preparer who handles international cases before filing.
