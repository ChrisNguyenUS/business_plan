Renewing a 10-year green card means filing **Form I-90 (Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card)** with U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS). According to USCIS, you should file when your card **has expired or will expire within the next 6 months**. The fee under the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026) is **$415 online** and **$465 on paper**. After you file, the USCIS receipt notice together with your old card serves as proof of permanent residence for **36 months** from the expiration date printed on the card.

You **stay a permanent resident** after your card expires; the card is only proof of your status. An expired card does make it harder to prove you can work, board a flight or reenter the U.S.

> Note: if you have a **2-year green card** (a conditional card, usually through marriage), you do **not** renew it with Form I-90. You file Form I-751 to remove the conditions. See [2-Year to 10-Year Green Card: Removing Conditions with Form I-751](/en/blog/remove-conditions-green-card-i-751).

## When do I need to file Form I-90?

According to the USCIS [Replace Your Green Card](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/replace-your-green-card) page (checked October 19, 2026), permanent residents need a new card when:

- The card **has expired or will expire within the next 6 months** (renewal).
- The card was **lost, stolen, mutilated or destroyed** (see [Lost Your Green Card?](/en/blog/lost-green-card)).
- The card has **incorrect information because of a USCIS error**, or you have **legally changed your name or other details**.
- You have reached your 14th birthday and your card was issued before then (unless it expires before your 16th birthday).
- You never received the card USCIS mailed.

If you are abroad and your card is about to expire, USCIS says to file Form I-90 **as soon as you return to the U.S.** If your card will expire during a trip, renew it before you leave. See also: [How Long Can a Green Card Holder Stay Outside the U.S.?](/en/blog/green-card-travel-how-long-abroad).

## How much does it cost to renew a green card?

According to the [USCIS G-1055 Fee Schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) (edition October 16, 2026, checked October 19, 2026):

| How you file | Form I-90 fee |
|---|---|
| Online through a USCIS account | $415 |
| On paper, by mail | $465 |

Biometrics are included in the fee. Some cases are free, such as a card with a USCIS error. Applicants with low income can check fee waiver eligibility with Form I-912 on the [I-912 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-912). Fees can change, so check again on the day you file.

## Should I file Form I-90 online or on paper?

According to the [USCIS I-90 page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-90) (updated October 7, 2026):

- **Online:** create a USCIS account, fill out the form, upload documents and pay online. You can track the case, get notices and answer requests for evidence in the account. Filing online costs $50 less.
- **On paper:** use the 01/20/25 edition and mail it to the address on the I-90 page. USCIS creates an online account for you and mails instructions to access it.

## Documents you usually need

According to the Form I-90 instructions, a renewal usually needs:

- **A copy of your current green card** (front and back).
- If your name changed: the legal document showing the change (marriage certificate, divorce decree, court order).
- Your details: A-Number, address and travel information.

After you file, USCIS sends a **biometrics** appointment notice for an Application Support Center, if needed.

## How long does green card renewal take?

Processing times vary. Check the current time on the [USCIS Processing Times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) page (select Form I-90).

You still have proof of status while you wait: according to a [USCIS announcement](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/uscis-extends-green-card-validity-extension-to-36-months-for-green-card-renewals), since September 10, 2024 the **Form I-90 receipt notice (Form I-797) together with your expired card** proves permanent resident status for **36 months** from the expiration date on the card. Use them together for work and travel. If you have no card (for example, it was lost), USCIS may issue an **ADIT stamp** (temporary proof of permanent residence) after you file Form I-90; you request an appointment at a USCIS office through the USCIS Contact Center.

## Should I apply for citizenship instead?

If you have had your green card for 5 years (or 3 years if married to and living with a U.S. citizen), you may be eligible to file **Form N-400** for citizenship instead of only renewing. Eligibility, fees and the process are in [What Is Form N-400?](/en/blog/what-is-form-n-400) and [U.S. Citizenship Test 2026](/en/blog/us-citizenship-test-guide). Which path fits depends on your situation; if you have a criminal record or long trips abroad, talk to a licensed immigration attorney first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I lose permanent residence if my green card expires?

No. The green card is only proof of your status. An expired card does not end your permanent residence, but it makes working and traveling harder, so renew it with Form I-90.

### When should I file to renew my green card?

According to USCIS, when the card has expired or will expire within the next 6 months.

### How much does it cost to renew a 10-year green card?

Under the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026): $415 online, $465 on paper, biometrics included. Fees can change, so check again before you file.

### What do I use for work and travel while I wait for the new card?

According to USCIS, the Form I-90 receipt notice together with your expired card proves permanent resident status for 36 months from the expiration date on the card.

### Can I renew a 2-year green card with Form I-90?

No. A conditional (2-year) card cannot be renewed; you file Form I-751 to remove the conditions in the 90 days before the card expires.

### Can I file Form I-90 online myself?

Yes. Create a USCIS account and file online; it costs $50 less than filing on paper.

### My green card is about to expire while I'm abroad. What should I do?

USCIS says to file Form I-90 as soon as you return to the U.S. If the card is lost while you are abroad, see our article on lost green cards and Form I-131A.
