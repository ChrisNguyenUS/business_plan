An EB-3 offer shows scam warning signs when it:

- **guarantees** a green card or a timeline;
- **asks you to pay PERM costs** or deducts recruitment costs from your wages;
- makes you **pay to receive a job offer**;
- wants payment by **Western Union, MoneyGram, gift cards or cryptocurrency**, or to **an individual**;
- comes from someone who **isn't a lawyer** giving immigration legal advice.

USCIS and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) both warn about these patterns.

Information from USCIS's [Common Scams page](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/common-scams) and the [FTC's guide to avoiding immigration scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-immigration-scams-and-get-real-help), checked October 19, 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - No one can guarantee an EB-3 green card or a timeline.
> - Being asked to pay PERM costs or to pay for a job offer is a red flag.
> - USCIS never accepts Western Union, MoneyGram, PayPal or gift cards.

## 8 signs to stop and check

### 1. "Guaranteed green card" or "guaranteed in X years"

No one can guarantee it. The Department of Labor decides PERM, USCIS decides the I-140, and the State Department follows the Visa Bulletin. USCIS also notes that only USCIS decides whether to expedite a case; anyone promising speed through "connections" is suspect.

### 2. The worker is asked to pay PERM costs

Under 20 CFR 656.12(b), the employer **may not take money from the worker** for PERM, including the employer's attorney fees. Wage deductions, unpaid work and "thank-you money" are banned too. See [EB-3 Costs: Who Pays What](/en/blog/eb3-costs-who-pays).

### 3. Paying to receive a job offer

USCIS warns to be careful with U.S. job offers sent by email or social media, especially when you're asked to pay to receive the offer.

### 4. Paying through unofficial channels

USCIS **never** asks you to send money to an individual and **doesn't accept** Western Union, MoneyGram, PayPal, Venmo or gift cards for immigration fees. The FTC adds that anyone asking for payment by gift card, wire transfer, payment app or cryptocurrency for "immigration help" is a scammer.

### 5. A "notario" or unlicensed person giving legal advice

In the U.S., a notary public witnesses signatures and **may not** give legal advice. According to USCIS, only an **attorney** or an **accredited representative** of an organization recognized by the Department of Justice may give immigration legal advice. Check a lawyer through the state bar where they're licensed.

### 6. Asked to sign blank forms or hand over originals

Don't sign blank forms or forms with false information. Don't let anyone keep your passport or original documents. Keep copies of everything filed and every USCIS receipt.

### 7. No contract, no clear company name

A legitimate provider has a written contract, a legal entity name and an address, and tells you the employer's name before PERM is filed. You have every right to check that the employer is real.

### 8. Fake USCIS websites or emails

U.S. government websites and emails end in **.gov**. Social media pages or emails claiming to be USCIS may be impostors.

## How to check an EB-3 provider

- Ask for the **name of the immigration attorney** on the case and check the license on the state bar's site.
- Ask for the **employer's name**, address and industry, and confirm the company actually operates.
- Ask for an **itemized cost list** and a **contract**.
- Pay only to a **company account**, with receipts.
- Get **copies** of everything filed with the government, with receipt numbers you can look up yourself.

## Where to report a scam

- FTC: [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).
- USCIS: its Report Immigration Scams page. USCIS says reporting a scam **will not hurt** your application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone guarantee an EB-3 green card?

No. The Department of Labor, USCIS and the State Department each decide a step. A guarantee of results or timing is a red flag.

### What if I'm asked to pay for PERM?

Under 20 CFR 656.12(b), the employer may not receive payment from the worker for PERM. Stop and ask a licensed immigration attorney.

### Does USCIS accept Western Union or PayPal?

No. USCIS doesn't accept Western Union, MoneyGram, PayPal, Venmo or gift cards, and never asks you to send money to an individual.

### Can a notary give immigration advice?

No. In the U.S., a notary public only witnesses signatures. Only attorneys or accredited representatives of DOJ-recognized organizations may give immigration legal advice.

### Will reporting a scam hurt my case?

According to USCIS, reporting a scam will not hurt your application.

### Where do I check an immigration attorney?

On the state bar website for the state where the attorney is licensed; it shows whether the license is active and any discipline.
