Driver's licenses in the U.S. are issued by **each state**, so fees, tests and DMV (or equivalent agency) hours differ from state to state. But one **federal** rule affects everyone: **REAL ID**. This article covers the federal part that applies across the U.S. For tests, fees and appointments, check the website of the licensing agency in your state.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Since **May 7, 2025**, anyone **18 or older** needs a **REAL ID**-compliant license or ID card, or another accepted ID, to board a domestic flight.
> - You **don't need a REAL ID to drive**. It is mainly for domestic flights and some federal facilities.
> - A **passport** works instead of a REAL ID for domestic flights.
> - If you arrive at the airport without an accepted ID, TSA says you'll be charged **$45**.
> - To get a REAL ID, the DMV usually asks for your **full legal name, date of birth, Social Security number, two proofs of address, and proof of lawful status** (citizen or lawfully present).

## What is REAL ID?

REAL ID is a federal security standard for state-issued licenses and ID cards. A compliant card has a marking (often a star) near the top. According to TSA, enforcement began on **May 7, 2025**, and since then people **18 or older** need an accepted ID to board a commercial aircraft within the U.S.

Accepted IDs include a REAL ID-compliant license or ID card, a passport, and others on [TSA's list of accepted identification](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/identification). If you are a permanent resident or hold other immigration documents, check that list.

## What to bring to the DMV

DHS says a REAL ID applicant must show:

- **Full legal name and date of birth** (often proven with a passport, birth certificate or green card).
- **Social Security number (SSN).** Under the REAL ID Modernization Act, states **no longer have to require** documents proving your SSN, but they must still collect the number. Some states still ask for a document showing it.
- **Two proofs of your principal residence**, such as a lease, utility bill, bank statement or mortgage paperwork.
- **Lawful status in the U.S.** Noncitizens commonly use a valid green card (Form I-551) or another accepted immigration document.

**Each state has its own detailed rules.** Before you go, check your state's document list and book an appointment if your state allows it. Bring **originals**. If the names on your documents differ (for example after a name change at marriage), bring the marriage certificate or name-change order. See [Changing Your Name After Marriage or Divorce](/en/blog/change-name-after-marriage-divorce-us).

## What if you don't have a REAL ID?

- **If you have a passport:** bring it when you fly within the U.S.
- **If you have no accepted ID:** TSA says travelers without acceptable ID must pay **$45** to use an identity verification service, and may not be allowed to fly.
- **For everyday driving:** a non-REAL ID license still works for driving.

## What order should a newcomer follow?

1. **Get an SSN** (if eligible). See [Getting a Social Security Number](/en/blog/get-social-security-number-replace-card).
2. **Get proof of address**: a lease or a bill in your name.
3. **Book an appointment at your state's DMV** and ask which card is REAL ID-compliant.
4. **Bring originals** of your passport, immigration papers, SSN and proof of address.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a REAL ID to drive?

No. REAL ID is mainly used to board domestic flights and enter some federal facilities. A regular license still works for driving.

### Since when do I need a REAL ID to fly?

Since May 7, 2025, people 18 or older need a REAL ID or another accepted ID to board a commercial flight within the U.S.

### Can I use a passport instead of a REAL ID?

Yes. TSA says a passport can be used instead for domestic flights.

### What documents do I need for a REAL ID?

Usually proof of your name and date of birth, your SSN, two proofs of address and proof of lawful status. Each state has its own list, so check your state's website.

### What happens at the airport without a REAL ID?

TSA says travelers without acceptable ID must pay $45 to use an identity verification service, and may not be allowed to fly.

### Is a green card accepted for getting a REAL ID?

A valid green card is commonly used to show identity and lawful status when applying for a REAL ID. Ask your state's DMV what else it requires.
