> **Updated October 22, 2026**
> The IRS published the **tax year 2026** figures on October 9, 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). They apply to returns filed in **2027**. As of this writing we have not seen official IRS figures for tax year 2027.

**Tax year 2026** covers income you earn from January 1 to December 31, 2026. You file in early 2027, and the deadline is normally **April 15, 2027** (a Thursday). Starting now saves stress: you still have more than two months to adjust your income, fund a retirement account or fix your paycheck withholding.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **Deadline:** April 15, 2027. An extension (Form 4868) moves the deadline to **file**, not the deadline to **pay**.
> - **2026 standard deduction:** $16,100 (single), $32,200 (married filing jointly), $24,150 (head of household).
> - **New deductions (tax years 2025 through 2028):** tips up to $25,000, overtime pay up to $12,500 ($25,000 if filing jointly), an extra $6,000 for people 65 and older, and car loan interest up to $10,000. Most require a **Social Security number (SSN)** on the return.
> - **Child Tax Credit:** up to $2,200 per qualifying child, and the child needs an SSN that is valid for employment.
> - **Parents or children in Vietnam** who are not U.S. citizens, residents or nationals cannot be claimed as dependents.

## Who has to file a federal return?

The IRS says **U.S. citizens and resident aliens are taxed on their worldwide income**. That means wages, bank interest or rent from a home in Vietnam all go on Form 1040 (or Form 1040-SR for seniors), just like U.S. income. If you are self-employed and your net earnings are **$400 or more**, you also figure self-employment tax on Schedule SE.

Not sure you are required to file? You should still file if you want a **refund**, because tax withheld from your pay only comes back if you file a return.

## The tax year 2026 numbers

### Standard deduction and tax brackets

| | Tax year 2025 | Tax year 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Single or married filing separately | $15,750 | **$16,100** |
| Married filing jointly | $31,500 | **$32,200** |
| Head of household | $23,625 | **$24,150** |

The first three brackets for tax year 2026, applied to taxable income (after the deduction):

| Rate | Single | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | up to $12,400 | up to $24,800 |
| 12% | over $12,400, up to $50,400 | over $24,800, up to $100,800 |
| 22% | over $50,400 | over $100,800 |

Higher income is taxed at 24% through 37%. The maximum EITC for a family with three or more children is $8,231. Source: IRS news release IR-2025-103.

### New deductions from the "One Big Beautiful Bill"

The law was signed July 4, 2025. The items below apply to tax years **2025 through 2028**. You can take them even if you do **not** itemize.

| Item | Maximum | Starts to phase out when income (MAGI) exceeds |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified tips | $25,000 | $150,000 ($300,000 if filing jointly) |
| Overtime pay (the premium part) | $12,500 ($25,000 if filing jointly) | $150,000 ($300,000 if filing jointly) |
| Age 65 or older | extra $6,000 per person ($12,000 if both spouses qualify) | $75,000 ($150,000 if filing jointly) |
| Car loan interest | $10,000 per year | $100,000 ($200,000 if filing jointly) |

According to the IRS, the **tips, overtime and senior deductions all require you to put your Social Security number on the return.** The car loan interest deduction requires the vehicle's VIN: the loan must have started after December 31, 2024, the car must be for personal use, final assembly must be in the U.S., and the loan must be secured by a lien. Leases don't qualify.

**Nail technicians and hair professionals, take note.** Treasury and the IRS issued final regulations on the tip deduction in April 2026. The list of eligible jobs includes the *Personal Appearance and Wellness* group, which covers **nail technicians** and barbers, according to summaries from [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2026/no-tax-tips-final-rules-confirm-qualifying-occupations-tip-definition.html) and [Greenshades](https://go.greenshades.com/blog/no-tax-on-tips-occupation-list). Only **tips customers pay voluntarily** (cash, card or transfer) count; an automatic service charge added to the bill does not. Starting with 2026 pay, employers must report your **occupation code (TTOC)** on your W-2. Check that your W-2 shows it, and keep a daily tip log.

### Child Tax Credit and dependents

- **Child Tax Credit:** "up to $2,200" per child under 17 at the end of the tax year. You (or your spouse, if filing jointly) and each child need an **SSN that is valid for employment** (per the IRS Child Tax Credit page). The refundable part (Additional Child Tax Credit) is up to $1,700 per child and requires at least $2,500 of earned income. The full credit applies if income is no more than $200,000 ($400,000 if filing jointly).
- **Children with an ITIN:** not eligible for the Child Tax Credit. They may qualify for the **Credit for Other Dependents**, up to $500 each.
- **A dependent must be a U.S. citizen, resident alien, U.S. national, or a resident of Canada or Mexico.** So parents or children **living in Vietnam** who don't fall into those groups cannot be your dependents, even if you send them money every month.

For more on spouses and children in Vietnam, see [First U.S. Tax Return for New Immigrants](/en/blog/first-us-tax-return-new-immigrants).

| Date | What |

**If you haven't filed your tax year 2025 return:** the extended deadline passed on October 15, 2026. File as soon as you can, because penalties and interest build with time. If you are due a refund, you should still file.

## Documents to gather

- **Photo ID** and the **SSN or ITIN** for you, your spouse and each dependent. Without a number, that person can't be claimed.
- **W-2** from each employer, and **1099s** (NEC, INT, DIV, G, K and others).
- **Form 1098** (mortgage interest, student loan interest) if you have it.
- **Last year's return**, for reference figures and bank details.
- **Bank account details** (routing and account number) if you want your refund by direct deposit.
- **Income and accounts in Vietnam** (savings interest, rent, taxes paid in Vietnam), if any.
- **Expense records** if you are self-employed or run a shop: invoices, statements, mileage.
- **Form 1095-A** if you bought health insurance through the Marketplace.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the deadline for the tax year 2026 return?

Normally April 15, 2027. If you request an extension with Form 4868, the deadline to file moves to October 15, 2027, but you still need to pay any tax you owe by April 15, 2027 to avoid interest and penalties.

### What is the standard deduction for 2026?

$16,100 for single filers or married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household, according to the IRS.

### Are overtime and tips tax-free?

Not entirely. The law gives you a **deduction** that reduces taxable income: tips up to $25,000 and overtime up to $12,500 ($25,000 if filing jointly) for 2025 through 2028, phasing out as income rises past the limits. You still pay Social Security and Medicare tax on these amounts, and you need an SSN on the return.

### My child lives in Vietnam. Can I claim the Child Tax Credit?

No, unless the child is a U.S. citizen or resident. The child needs an SSN valid for employment and must be an eligible dependent (a U.S. citizen, resident alien, U.S. national, or a resident of Canada or Mexico).

### Do I have to report income and accounts in Vietnam?

Yes, if you are a U.S. citizen or resident. Income from Vietnam goes on Form 1040. Bank accounts in Vietnam may also need to be reported on the FBAR and Form 8938 (see the FBAR and FATCA article above).

### Has the IRS announced the tax year 2027 figures?

Not as of October 22, 2026, as far as we can see. The IRS usually publishes them in the fall. We will update this article when it does.

### What if I haven't filed my tax year 2025 return yet?

The extended deadline for tax year 2025 was October 15, 2026, so it has passed. File as soon as you can. If you are due a refund there is no late-filing penalty, but filing sooner still avoids trouble.
