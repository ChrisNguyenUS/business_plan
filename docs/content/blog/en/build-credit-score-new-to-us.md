In the U.S., your **credit score** affects renting an apartment, getting a car loan or mortgage, and even signing up for some services. People new to the U.S. usually have **no credit history**, so no score. That is common: the CFPB estimates about **26 million U.S. adults** (roughly 1 in 10) have no credit record. This article explains how to start safely. It is general information, not personal financial advice.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **A credit score is calculated from your credit report**, kept by three companies (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). If you haven't borrowed or used a card, you often have no file.
> - You can check **your credit reports free every week** at **AnnualCreditReport.com**, the only authorized source.
> - The CFPB stresses that **checking and fixing errors on your report** matters more than just looking at the score.
> - Common ways to build credit history: a **secured credit card** and a **credit builder loan**.
> - **There's no fast, legal way to "erase" accurate negative information.** Anyone who promises quick credit repair is usually a scam.

## Where does a credit score come from?

A credit score is calculated from your **credit report**, a record of the cards and loans you have and whether you pay on time. Three companies keep these records: Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. If you don't have a card or loan in your name in the U.S., your file may be empty.

The CFPB notes that many people without a score still pay utility and phone bills on time. Some products consider **alternative payment data** such as rent and utilities, but not every lender uses it.

## Checking your credit reports for free

According to the FTC, the three credit reporting companies have **permanently extended** free **weekly** online access to your reports. By law you are also entitled to one free report every 12 months from each company. The FTC warns that **AnnualCreditReport.com** is the only authorized source for this service. Other sites may charge you or be fraudulent sites set up to steal your personal information.

You can request reports online (immediately), by phone at **1-877-322-8228**, or by mail (sent within 15 days).

**What to do:** once you have a file, check that your name, address and accounts are correct. If there is an error, you have the right to **dispute** it. The FTC explains how.

## Ways to start building credit history

The CFPB lists two products designed to **establish or rebuild credit history**:

- **Secured credit card:** you put down a deposit, and the card's limit is usually tied to it. If the issuer reports your payments to the credit reporting companies, on-time payments get recorded.
- **Credit builder loan:** you make payments **first**, and receive the money once you've paid. A CFPB study found this type is especially helpful for people with **no score** or **no other debt**, but less helpful for people who already carry debt.

Before signing up for any product, ask: **does it report to all three credit reporting companies?** If not, paying on time won't help. Also ask about **fees** and whether it accepts an **ITIN** instead of an SSN. Policies differ among issuers.

## Good habits

- **Pay on time.** Set reminders or autopay.
- **Don't max out your limit.** Keep your balance low relative to the limit.
- **Don't open many cards at once.**
- **Check your reports** a few times a year.
- **Keep a healthy bank account.** See [Opening a U.S. Bank Account for the First Time](/en/blog/open-bank-account-us-first-time).

## Avoid "credit repair" scams

The FTC has warned about illegal "credit repair" tricks spreading on social media. The rule of thumb: **a legitimate service can't remove accurate information** from your report, and you **shouldn't pay up front**. Don't buy a Social Security number or a "new credit profile." If you need to change your name on your records, see [Changing Your Name After Marriage or Divorce](/en/blog/change-name-after-marriage-divorce-us).

## Frequently asked questions

### I just arrived in the U.S. Why don't I have a credit score?

A score is calculated from loan and payment records in your name in the U.S. With no card or loan, you often have no file. That is common: the CFPB estimates about 26 million U.S. adults have no credit record.

### Where can I see my credit report for free?

At AnnualCreditReport.com, the only authorized source. According to the FTC, the three credit reporting companies offer free reports every week.

### What is a secured credit card?

A credit card backed by a deposit you make, often used to build credit history. Ask the issuer whether it reports to all three credit reporting companies.

### What is a credit builder loan?

A loan where you make payments first and get the money once you've paid. The CFPB found it more helpful for people with no score or no other debt.

### Can I use an ITIN instead of an SSN to build credit?

It depends on the lender. Some accept an ITIN and others don't. Ask before you apply.

### Can a service quickly erase bad marks?

A legitimate service can't remove accurate information from your report. You can only dispute inaccurate information. Be wary of promises of quick credit repair or requests for payment up front.
