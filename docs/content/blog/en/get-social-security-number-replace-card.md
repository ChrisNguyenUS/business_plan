A **Social Security number (SSN)** is a 9-digit number that stays with you for life in the U.S. You need it to work, file taxes, open a bank account, borrow money, get a passport or claim government benefits. Getting an SSN and replacing a card is **completely free** when you deal directly with the Social Security Administration (SSA). This article covers the general process across the U.S., because SSA is a federal agency.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **A new SSN and a replacement card are both free.** Be wary of anyone who charges a fee to "do it for you."
> - If you are in the U.S., you **apply online, then go to an SSA office** to show your documents. The card arrives by mail in **5 to 10 business days**.
> - **Every U.S. citizen** can get an SSN. Noncitizens can get one only in certain cases: they can work, they attend school, or they have a valid reason unrelated to work.
> - SSA **does not accept photocopies or notarized copies**. You need originals or copies certified by the issuing agency.
> - If you know your number, you **usually don't need to replace the card**. A paper card is rarely required.

## Who can get an SSN?

SSA says all U.S. citizens can request an SSN. Noncitizens can request one if they **work or attend school** in the U.S., or if they have a **valid reason that isn't related to work**. SSA has separate guidance for immigrants, international students, foreign workers and U.S.-citizen children.

People who **aren't eligible for an SSN** but still must file taxes use an **ITIN** from the IRS, not an SSN. See [Your First U.S. Tax Return](/en/blog/first-us-tax-return-new-immigrants).

## Getting an SSN for the first time

1. **Start your application online** on SSA's website.
2. **Take your documents to an SSA office** near you. SSA needs proof of your **identity, age, and U.S. citizenship or lawful immigration status**. The exact list depends on your situation, so check [SSA's required documents page](https://www.ssa.gov/ssnumber/ss5doc.htm) before you go.
3. **Wait for the card by mail.** Once your application is approved, the card arrives in 5 to 10 business days.

**For a newborn**, the easiest route is to apply for the SSN at the hospital when you register the birth. See [Having a Baby in the U.S.](/en/blog/having-a-baby-in-the-us-paperwork).

## Replacing a lost, stolen or damaged card

A replacement card is **free**, and SSA says the fastest way is to start your application online. Depending on your situation, you may be able to finish online. If not, you must make an appointment at an office. Local offices **can't print a card on the spot**; the card is always mailed, in 5 to 10 business days.

To prove your identity for a replacement, SSA accepts a current driver's license, a state-issued ID card or a U.S. passport. If you were born abroad, you may also need to prove citizenship or lawful work status if SSA doesn't already have it.

### Limits on replacement cards

Under SSA's internal guidance ([POMS RM 10205.400](https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0110205400)), each person can get at most **3 replacement cards per year** and **10 in a lifetime**. Legal name changes don't count toward the limit. If you hit a limit, you can still ask for an exception by showing significant hardship.

So **keep your card somewhere safe** and don't carry it in your wallet.

## Do I need to report a lost card to the police?

A lost card doesn't necessarily mean your identity was stolen. But if you suspect **someone else is using your number**, SSA says to report it to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). Only in rare cases can a victim of identity theft qualify for a **new number**.

## Contacting SSA

Call **1-800-772-1213** (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m., with language support beyond English). Use only official government sites that end in **.gov**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does it cost money to get a Social Security card?

No. SSA issues SSNs and replacement cards for free. Anyone who charges a fee to file for you is not SSA.

### I just arrived in the U.S. How do I get an SSN?

If you are allowed to work or attend school, you can start the application online and then bring original documents to an SSA office. The card is mailed 5 to 10 business days after approval.

### Does SSA accept photocopies of my documents?

No. SSA doesn't accept photocopies or notarized copies. You need originals or copies certified by the agency that issued them.

### I lost my SSN card. What do I do?

You request a free replacement, usually starting online. If you can't finish online, make an appointment at an SSA office. If you know your number, you often don't need the paper card.

### How many times can I replace my card?

At most 3 cards per year and 10 in a lifetime, according to SSA's guidance. Legal name changes don't count toward the limit.

### I'm not eligible for an SSN but I have to file taxes. What now?

You can apply for an ITIN from the IRS. An ITIN is for federal tax purposes only and doesn't give you the right to work.
