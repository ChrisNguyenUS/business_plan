Losing your green card **does not end your permanent resident status**: you only lose the proof. What to do depends on where you are:

- **Lost in the U.S.:** file **Form I-90** for a new card. The fee is $415 online or $465 on paper.
- **Lost abroad (in Vietnam, for example) on a trip of less than 1 year:** file **Form I-131A** at a U.S. consulate for a document to board your flight home. The fee is $575, paid online.

Fees are from the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026), checked October 19, 2026.

## Lost your green card in the U.S.

According to the USCIS [Replace Your Green Card](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/replace-your-green-card) page (checked October 19, 2026), a card that was **lost, stolen, mutilated or destroyed** is replaced with **Form I-90**, filed online or by mail.

The usual steps:

1. **If it was stolen**, consider filing a police report and keep a copy. A green card can be used for identity theft.
2. **File Form I-90**: $415 online or $465 on paper, under the [G-1055 Fee Schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055). Filing online is simpler and $50 cheaper.
3. **Attend biometrics** if USCIS schedules an appointment.
4. **Receive the new card** by mail. Track the case in your USCIS account.

### What do I use while I wait?

USCIS says it may issue an **ADIT stamp** (Alien Documentation, Identification & Telecommunications) after you file Form I-90. It is a temporary stamp in your passport that proves permanent residence. Contact the USCIS Contact Center to request an appointment at a USCIS office. This helps if you need to prove status for work or must travel urgently.

If your card was lost **and** is about to expire, one Form I-90 both replaces and renews it. See [Renewing a 10-Year Green Card (Form I-90)](/en/blog/renew-green-card-i-90).

> A lost 2-year (conditional) card is also replaced with Form I-90. But if your 2-year card is **about to expire**, you must file **Form I-751**, not Form I-90. See [2-Year to 10-Year Green Card](/en/blog/remove-conditions-green-card-i-751).

## Lost your green card abroad

According to the [USCIS I-131A page](https://www.uscis.gov/i-131a) (updated June 3, 2026):

- You can file **Form I-131A (Application for Carrier Documentation)** if your green card was **lost, stolen or destroyed** while you were on temporary travel of **less than 1 year**, or **less than 2 years** if you have a reentry permit and that permit was lost.
- **Pay the fee online first** through USCIS's online payment system. The fee is $575 under the G-1055 Fee Schedule; it is non-refundable and **cannot be waived**.
- **File in person** with the consular section of a U.S. Embassy or Consulate, bringing proof of payment. USCIS advises **checking first** that the embassy or consulate accepts Form I-131A before you pay.
- If approved, you receive documentation that lets an airline board you for the U.S. (carrier documentation). Once back in the U.S., file Form I-90 for a new card.

If you have been abroad **more than 1 year** without a reentry permit, or more than 2 years even with one, Form I-131A doesn't apply. You may need a returning resident (SB-1) visa. See [How Long Can a Green Card Holder Stay Outside the U.S.?](/en/blog/green-card-travel-how-long-abroad).

## Quick comparison

| Situation | Form | Fee (G-1055, 10/16/26) | Where to file |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lost in the U.S. | I-90 | $415 online / $465 paper | USCIS (online or by mail) |
| Lost abroad on a trip under 1 year | I-131A | $575 (paid online) | In person at a U.S. Embassy / Consulate |
| Abroad too long (over 1 year, no reentry permit) | SB-1 visa | Department of State fees | U.S. Embassy / Consulate |

## How to protect yourself before it happens

- **Keep a copy of both sides of your green card**, with your A-Number. It makes replacement faster.
- **Don't carry the card when you don't need it** within the U.S., but **always carry it when you travel abroad**. The law requires permanent residents 18 and older to carry proof of status, so ask a qualified professional if you're unsure.
- **Consider applying for citizenship** if you qualify. U.S. citizens use a U.S. passport and no longer need a green card. See [U.S. Citizenship Test 2026](/en/blog/us-citizenship-test-guide) and practice free with [N400 Ready](/en/n400ready).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I lose permanent residence if I lose my green card?

No. The card is only proof. You are still a permanent resident and replace the card with Form I-90.

### How long does it take to replace a lost green card?

It varies; check the USCIS Processing Times page (Form I-90). While you wait, USCIS may issue an ADIT stamp in your passport as proof of permanent residence.

### How much does it cost to replace a lost green card?

Under the G-1055 Fee Schedule (edition October 16, 2026): $415 to file Form I-90 online, $465 on paper.

### I lost my green card in Vietnam. How do I get back to the U.S.?

If your trip was under 1 year, pay the $575 fee online and then file Form I-131A in person at a U.S. consulate for a document to board your flight. Once home, file Form I-90 for a new card.

### Can the Form I-131A fee be waived?

No. According to USCIS, the Form I-131A fee cannot be waived and is not refundable.

### Does the U.S. Consulate in Ho Chi Minh City accept Form I-131A?

USCIS advises checking with the embassy or consulate that it processes Form I-131A before paying. Contact the consular section to confirm.

### Do I need a police report for a stolen green card?

It isn't required to file Form I-90, but a police report helps protect you if the card is used for identity theft.
