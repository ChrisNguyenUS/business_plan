> **What changed on September 11, 2026**
> Many guides still tell you to send your papers to the Vietnamese Embassy or a Vietnamese Consulate in the U.S. for legalization. Since **September 11, 2026**, Vietnam applies the Apostille Convention. U.S. public documents need only a state **apostille**, and Vietnamese offices may **not** ask for legalization on top of it (Article 25, Decree 293/2026/ND-CP). Marriage applications also go to the **commune-level People's Committee**, not the old district justice office.

To register a marriage in Vietnam with a Vietnamese citizen, the partner from abroad must prove that they are not currently married. Vietnamese-Americans call this paperwork **"công hàm độc thân"**; Vietnamese law calls it "proof of marital status". You need it if you are a **U.S. citizen** (of Vietnamese origin or not) or a **green card holder** and plan to marry in Vietnam.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The U.S. has no single national "certificate of single status". You use a **no-marriage-record letter from a County Clerk or state Vital Records office**, and/or an **Affidavit of Single Status** notarized in the U.S., then get an **apostille**.
> - **Vietnamese-American U.S. citizens must submit a No Marriage Record letter** from a County Clerk or Vital Records office, with an apostille. An affidavit signed at the U.S. Embassy does not replace it.
> - U.S. citizens not of Vietnamese origin who are already in Vietnam can sign the affidavit at the **U.S. Embassy in Hanoi or the Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City** for **$50**.
> - If they show no expiry date, the single-status proof and the mental health certificate are valid for **6 months**.
> - **One partner can file alone**, without a power of attorney, and online filing is allowed. Processing takes **5 working days** (up to 10 if verification is needed).
> - **Both partners must appear** to receive the marriage certificate.

## What is proof of single status for a marriage in Vietnam?

Under Article 38(1) of Vietnam's 2014 Law on Civil Status, **foreigners and Vietnamese citizens living abroad** must submit proof of marital status to register a marriage in Vietnam. In the U.S., each state or county records marriages, and no office certifies marital status for the whole country. So the "proof" from the U.S. is usually **a set of documents**, not one paper.

Article 30 of Decree 123/2015/ND-CP allows for this: if a country does not issue marital status certificates, the applicant submits a document from that country's competent authority confirming they meet the conditions to marry under its law.

| You are | Where to get the single-status proof | Notes |

| **Green card holder** | **In the U.S.**: County Clerk/Vital Records + apostille | The U.S. Embassy's marriage guidance is written for U.S. citizens. You also need your U.S. residence document (green card) |

## Option 1: Get your single-status proof in the U.S.

This works for both U.S. citizens and green card holders.

### Step 1. Request a no-marriage-record letter

Offices call it a *Certificate of No Marriage Record*, *No Record Found* letter or *Single Status Letter*. It comes from the **County Clerk** or the state **Vital Records** office where you live.

- Search "[county name] County Clerk" or "[state name] Vital Records". Some offices require an online appointment.
- Bring a valid photo ID (driver's license, state ID or passport).
- Search period: the U.S. Embassy in Vietnam describes a search from **age 18** to today. If you were divorced or widowed, the search often starts from that date. If you lived in several states, ask whether the office can search outside its own county; many can't.
- Ask for a **certified copy** so it can be apostilled.

### Step 2. Sign an Affidavit of Single Status before a notary (if needed)

Many applications also include an **Affidavit of Single Status** signed before a **notary public** in the U.S. Ask the commune office in advance whether it wants the County Clerk letter, the affidavit, or both.

### Step 3. Get an apostille

Take the County Clerk/Vital Records letter and the notarized affidavit to the **Secretary of State** of the state that issued the record or where the notary is commissioned. Some federal documents are apostilled by the State Department's **Office of Authentications**. See the State Department's [apostille requirements](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/records-and-authentications/authenticate-your-document/apostille-requirements.html).

Vietnam deposited its accession to the Apostille Convention on December 31, 2025, and the Convention took effect for Vietnam on **September 11, 2026** ([HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41)). The U.S. Embassy in Vietnam also states that both countries are parties. Apostilles issued **before** September 11, 2026 are still accepted unless a refusal ground applies (Article 36, Decree 293/2026/ND-CP).

### Step 4. Divorce decree or death certificate (if any)

If you were married before, get a certified copy of the **divorce decree** from the court, or the **death certificate** of your former spouse, then an **apostille** from that state's Secretary of State.

If you are a Vietnamese citizen who divorced in the U.S., Vietnam's civil status database may not show it yet. The commune office will then ask you to **record the foreign divorce** in the Vietnamese civil register before the marriage (Article 30(3), Decree 123/2015/ND-CP).

### Step 5. Translate into Vietnamese

Any apostilled document not in Vietnamese must be **translated into Vietnamese, and the translation notarized or certified** under Vietnamese law (Article 27, Decree 293/2026/ND-CP). A notary office in Vietnam can do this.

## Option 2: U.S. citizens not of Vietnamese origin sign the affidavit at the U.S. Embassy in Vietnam

This option **does not work for Vietnamese-Americans**: U.S. citizens of Vietnamese origin still need a No Marriage Record letter from the U.S. (Option 1).

If you are a U.S. citizen not of Vietnamese origin and already in Vietnam, the U.S. Embassy's [Notarials](https://vn.usembassy.gov/services/notarials/) and [Marriage](https://vn.usembassy.gov/marriage/) pages explain the process:

1. **Book a notarial appointment** at the Embassy in Hanoi or the Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City. Slots open about two weeks ahead.
2. **Download the "Affidavit of Single Status for marriage in Vietnam"** form and fill it in, but **don't sign it**. You sign in front of the consular officer.
3. **Bring your U.S. passport.** If you were married before, bring a certified divorce decree or death certificate.
4. **Pay $50 per seal** by card. Cash is accepted only in limited cases and must be the exact amount.

The Embassy says the affidavit must be signed **within six months** before you apply for the marriage certificate.

**Note:** an affidavit signed at the Embassy is executed by a consular officer, so it **cannot receive an apostille** (Article 26(1), Decree 293/2026/ND-CP). Under earlier practice, people took it to the **Consular Department (Hanoi) or the Ho Chi Minh City Department of Foreign Affairs** for legalization before filing. We have not seen guidance after September 11, 2026 that changes this. Ask the commune office whether it requires that step.

## What goes in the marriage application in Vietnam?

Per the Hung Yen Department of Justice handbook (applying Decree 123/2015/ND-CP and Decree 120/2025/ND-CP):

| Document | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage registration form | Both | One joint form is fine. Online filers complete the e-form |
| Medical certificate: no mental illness or other condition that prevents understanding and controlling one's actions | **Both** | Issued by a competent Vietnamese or foreign health facility. One issued in Vietnam is not a foreign document and needs no apostille |
| Proof of single status | Partner from the U.S. | See Option 1 or Option 2 |
| Copy of passport | Partner from the U.S. | Foreigners and Vietnamese citizens living abroad |
| U.S. residence document | Vietnamese citizens living in the U.S. | For example a green card |
| Divorce decree or death certificate | Anyone previously married | Apostilled and translated |
| Citizen ID card | Vietnamese partner | No upload needed if the system fills it from the population database |

**Six-month rule:** if the single-status proof shows no validity period, it and the mental health certificate are valid for **6 months from issue** (Article 30(1), Decree 123/2015/ND-CP). Get them last.

**Don't over-file:** the handbook says the receiving officer "may not require" documents that civil status law does not list. A biographic information sheet or "statement of non-impediment" is not on the list above.

## Where to file, how long it takes, and who must appear

- **Where:** the **commune-level People's Committee where the Vietnamese partner lives** (Article 4, Decree 120/2025/ND-CP). The U.S. Embassy page still says "District People's Committee"; that changed on July 1, 2025.
- **How:** in person at the Public Administrative Service Center, by post, or online at the [National Public Service Portal](https://dichvucong.gov.vn). **Either partner can file without a power of attorney from the other.** If you file online, you hand in the single-status proof and medical certificate when you collect the result.
- **How long:** **5 working days** from a complete application; **up to 10 working days** if verification is needed.
- **Pick-up:** marriage is the one civil status procedure where **both partners must appear**, show ID and sign the marriage register. If one of you can't come, ask in writing for an extension of **up to 60 days** from the date the certificate is signed. After 60 days the certificate is cancelled and you must start over.
- **Fee:** set by each province's People's Council. Ask the commune office.

## Common pitfalls

- **Short trip to Vietnam.** Let the Vietnamese partner file first, in person or online. The partner from the U.S. only needs to arrive for the pick-up date. Ask the office for the pick-up date before you buy tickets.
- **Six-month expiry.** Don't request the County Clerk letter or do the medical exam too early.
- **New immigrant without a green card yet.** Vietnamese citizens living abroad must hold a residence document issued abroad. Ask the commune office what it accepts while you wait for your card.
- **Lived in several states.** One County Clerk letter may cover only one county. Ask whether the office wants letters from every place you lived.
- **Marriage age.** Men must be at least **20** and women at least **18** (2014 Law on Marriage and Family).
- **Sham marriage.** Vietnamese law bans sham marriages. The office may interview you to confirm both partners consent and can communicate.

## After the wedding: sponsoring your spouse

A marriage legally registered in Vietnam is generally valid in the U.S., and you don't need to report it to the U.S. Embassy. The next step is **Form I-130** with USCIS. Spouses of U.S. citizens don't wait for a visa number; green card holders sponsor in the **F2A** category. See [Sponsoring Your Spouse for a Green Card](/en/blog/sponsor-spouse-green-card).

Keep the original marriage certificate, wedding photos, messages and travel records. You will need them as evidence of a genuine marriage.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the single-status paperwork take in the U.S.?

It depends on your County Clerk and Secretary of State. Dropping the Vietnamese Embassy step saves a lot of time compared with the old process. Ask each office for its processing time before you book your flight.

### Since September 11, 2026, do I still send documents to the Vietnamese Embassy or a Vietnamese Consulate in the U.S.?

No, not for U.S. public documents that carry an apostille. Article 37 of Decree 293/2026/ND-CP says Vietnamese missions abroad do not legalize documents exempted under the Apostille Convention.

### Can a Vietnamese-American sign the affidavit at the U.S. Embassy in Vietnam instead of getting papers in the U.S.?

No. U.S. citizens of Vietnamese origin and green card holders need a No Marriage Record letter from a County Clerk or Vital Records office, with an apostille (Option 1).

### Do I have to fly to Vietnam to file the application?

No. The Vietnamese partner can file alone without a power of attorney. You must be there to receive the marriage certificate.
