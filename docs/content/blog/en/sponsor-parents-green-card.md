Only **U.S. citizens who are 21 or older** can sponsor their parents for a green card. According to USCIS, parents of U.S. citizens are **immediate relatives**: "Visas are always available for immediate relatives of U.S. citizens", so there is **no wait for a visa number** as in the preference categories. **Green card holders cannot sponsor parents**: the USCIS list of relatives a permanent resident may petition for includes only a spouse and unmarried children.

Each parent needs a **separate Form I-130**. The fee is $625 online or $675 on paper (G-1055 Fee Schedule, edition October 16, 2026).

## Who can sponsor a parent?

According to USCIS [Family of U.S. Citizens](https://www.uscis.gov/family/family-of-us-citizens) (checked October 19, 2026), immediate relatives of U.S. citizens are:

- Spouses of U.S. citizens.
- Unmarried children under 21.
- **Parents of U.S. citizens** (the petitioner must be **21 or older**).

That means:

- You must **be a U.S. citizen** when you file. If you have a green card, the path is to **naturalize first**, then sponsor your parents. See [U.S. Citizenship Test 2026](/en/blog/us-citizenship-test-guide) and [What Is Form N-400?](/en/blog/what-is-form-n-400).
- You must **be at least 21**.
- The parent-child relationship must be documented, usually with **your birth certificate showing your parent's name**. The Form I-130 instructions list extra requirements for some cases, such as a father petitioning for a child born out of wedlock, or a stepparent (married before you turned 18).

## What are the steps?

If your parents are abroad (consular processing):

1. **File Form I-130** for each parent (two separate petitions). Include proof of your U.S. citizenship and your birth certificate. For a father, include your parents' marriage certificate in most cases.
2. **USCIS approves Form I-130** and sends the case to the **National Visa Center (NVC)**.
3. **Pay NVC fees** for each parent: the **$325** immigrant visa application fee, plus the **$120** Affidavit of Support review fee, per the [State Department fee page](https://adoption.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html).
4. **Submit Form DS-260, your parents' civil documents, and Form I-864.** You are the financial sponsor; USCIS charges no fee for Form I-864.
5. **Your parents have a medical exam and an interview** at the U.S. embassy or consulate handling immigrant visas for their country. For Vietnam, that is the U.S. Consulate General in Ho Chi Minh City.
6. **They enter the U.S.** and receive 10-year green cards by mail.

If your parents are lawfully in the U.S. (on a visitor visa, for example), adjustment of status with **Form I-485** ($1,390 online / $1,440 paper) may be an option. It depends on many conditions, such as how they entered and the purpose stated when they applied for the visa. Talk to a licensed immigration attorney before choosing this path.

## How long does it take?

Because parents are immediate relatives, **there is no wait for a visa number**. The timeline includes:

- USCIS processing of Form I-130: see [USCIS Processing Times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/).
- NVC processing and the interview schedule at the consulate.

Timelines vary over time; there is no single number that applies to every case.

## Total government fees (per parent, visa processed abroad)

According to the [USCIS G-1055 Fee Schedule](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) (edition October 16, 2026) and the [State Department fee page](https://adoption.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/fees-visa-services.html) (checked October 19, 2026):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Form I-130 (USCIS) | $625 online / $675 paper |
| Immigrant visa application fee (State Department) | $325 |
| Affidavit of Support review fee | $120 |
| Form I-864 (USCIS) | $0 |

This does not include the medical exam, translations or documents obtained abroad. Fees can change, so check again before you file.

## Does the sponsor need enough income?

Yes. You sign **Form I-864**, promising financial support for your parents. Your income must meet the minimum in USCIS guidelines, generally 125% of the federal poverty guidelines for your household size, **counting the parents you sponsor**. If you fall short, you can use assets or a joint sponsor. This promise is legally binding, so read the Form I-864 instructions carefully.

## After your parents arrive

- Your parents receive **10-year green cards** and can live and work in the U.S.
- After 5 years as permanent residents, they can apply for citizenship. Applicants 55 or older who have had a green card long enough may test in their own language. See [The Citizenship Test for Applicants 65 and Older](/en/blog/citizenship-test-over-65).
- If they travel abroad for long periods, see [How Long Can a Green Card Holder Stay Outside the U.S.?](/en/blog/green-card-travel-how-long-abroad).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a green card holder sponsor their parents?

No. According to USCIS, permanent residents can only sponsor a spouse and unmarried children. To sponsor parents, you must be a U.S. citizen who is 21 or older.

### What category is a parent sponsored by a U.S. citizen?

The immediate relative category of a U.S. citizen. It does not wait for a visa number under the Visa Bulletin.

### How long does it take to sponsor parents?

There is no visa number wait; the timeline includes USCIS processing of Form I-130, NVC processing and the consulate's interview schedule. Check current times on the USCIS Processing Times page.

### Do I need two petitions to sponsor both parents?

Yes. Each parent needs a separate Form I-130 and a separate set of fees.

### What are the government fees to sponsor a parent?

Per parent: Form I-130 $625 online or $675 on paper, the $325 visa application fee and the $120 Affidavit of Support review fee, under the USCIS fee schedule (edition October 16, 2026) and the State Department. Fees can change.

### Can I sponsor my parents if my income is too low?

Possibly, if you make up the difference with assets or a qualifying joint sponsor who signs Form I-864.

### Can my parents take the citizenship test in Vietnamese later?

After 5 years as permanent residents, your parents can apply for citizenship. Only applicants who qualify for 50/20, 55/15 or 65/20 are exempt from English and can test in Vietnamese.
