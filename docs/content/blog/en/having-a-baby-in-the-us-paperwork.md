A child born in the U.S. is generally a **U.S. citizen** and needs a set of documents in the first weeks: a birth certificate, a Social Security number and health coverage. The hospital helps with most of it, but **some deadlines are only a few weeks long**, and missing them causes a lot more trouble. This article summarizes the general steps across the U.S. Details like fees and how to order copies vary by state.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **At the hospital:** you fill in the **birth registration** details and are asked whether you want to **apply for the baby's SSN** (the Enumeration at Birth program). It is **voluntary**.
> - The baby's SSN card usually arrives in **about 2 weeks of processing plus up to 2 weeks in the mail**. Processing runs from 1 to 6 weeks depending on the state.
> - **Employer coverage:** you must ask to add the baby within **30 days** of birth, and coverage starts **on the date of birth**.
> - **Marketplace coverage (healthcare.gov):** you have **60 days** after the birth to enroll, with coverage starting **on the date of birth**.
> - **Medicaid/CHIP:** you can apply **at any time of year**. Eligibility and steps vary by state.

## 1. At the hospital: birth registration and SSN

The hospital collects information to **register the birth** with the state vital records office. It also asks whether you want to apply for your **baby's SSN**. According to SSA, if you say yes, you'll be asked for **both parents' SSNs**. If you don't have both, you can still apply for your baby's SSN. You receive **Form SSA-2853** as a receipt.

**Why apply at the hospital?** SSA says if you wait, there may be delays while SSA verifies your child's birth certificate. The card usually arrives in about 4 weeks. If it hasn't come after that, contact your local SSA office.

**Commemorative 250th anniversary card.** SSA says babies born between **July 2 and December 31, 2026** will receive a card with a commemorative design. It works exactly like a regular card and is issued only through the Enumeration at Birth program.

For SSN details, see [Getting a Social Security Number and Replacing a Lost Card](/en/blog/get-social-security-number-replace-card).

## 2. The birth certificate

Registering the birth doesn't mean you already have a **certified copy**. You usually order that separately from the state vital records office and wait a few weeks. Order **a few copies**, because many procedures need a certified original, such as your baby's passport. See [U.S. Birth Certificates: How to Get a Certified Copy](/en/blog/us-birth-certificate-certified-copy).

## 3. Health coverage for the baby

This is the step with a **deadline**, so do it early.

- **Coverage through a job (group health plan):** according to the U.S. Department of Labor, you must ask to add the baby **within 30 days** of birth. If you do it on time, the baby's coverage is effective **from the date of birth**. Some states allow extra time if the plan is bought through an insurance company.
- **Coverage bought through the Marketplace (healthcare.gov):** a birth gives you a special enrollment period. According to healthcare.gov, you have **60 days** after the birth to enroll, and coverage starts **on the date of birth**.
- **Medicaid and CHIP:** you can apply **at any time**. According to CMS, if the mother had Medicaid when she gave birth, the baby is usually covered by Medicaid **automatically**. Specific rules vary by state.

## 4. A passport for the baby

If you plan to travel to Vietnam or abroad, the baby needs their own passport. Children under 16 apply **in person** with **both parents** present. See [Getting Your First U.S. Passport](/en/blog/first-us-passport-fees-documents).

## Checklist

1. Fill in the **birth registration** at the hospital and choose to **apply for the SSN**.
2. Keep the **SSA-2853 receipt**.
3. **Add the baby to insurance** within 30 or 60 days, depending on the type of coverage.
4. **Order certified copies** of the birth certificate (a few).
5. Watch for the SSN card and contact SSA if it's late.
6. When you need to travel abroad, get the baby a passport.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the easiest way to get an SSN for my baby?

At the hospital, when you fill in the birth registration details. The Enumeration at Birth program is voluntary and usually faster than applying later.

### How long does the baby's SSN card take?

SSA says processing averages about 2 weeks, plus up to 2 more weeks for the card to arrive by mail. States take from 1 to 6 weeks to process. If it takes longer, contact your SSA office.

### Can I apply for my baby's SSN if I don't have an SSN myself?

Possibly. SSA says that if you don't have both parents' numbers, you can still apply for the baby's SSN.

### How long do I have to add my baby to my employer's plan?

According to the U.S. Department of Labor, you must ask within 30 days of birth. If you do so on time, the baby's coverage is effective from the date of birth.

### How long do I have if I buy insurance on healthcare.gov?

60 days after the birth, and coverage starts on the date the baby was born.

### Does my baby automatically get Medicaid?

If the mother had Medicaid when she gave birth, the baby is usually covered automatically. In other cases you can apply for Medicaid or CHIP at any time, depending on your state's rules.
