Open Enrollment is the yearly window to buy, change or renew health insurance on the Marketplace (the exchange created by the Affordable Care Act, also called Obamacare). On HealthCare.gov, the window for 2027 coverage runs from **November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027**. Enroll by **December 15, 2026** and pay your first premium, and your coverage starts **January 1, 2027**.

Compare your options this year even if you already have a plan. A new law narrows which immigrants can get savings starting January 1, 2027, and if you underestimate your income, you may have to repay all of the extra savings when you file taxes.

> **Quick summary**
> - **Deadline:** December 15, 2026 for January 1, 2027 coverage. Enroll from December 16 to January 15 and coverage starts February 1, 2027.
> - **2027 savings:** household income generally has to fall between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level. The 400% limit is $63,840 for one person and $132,000 for a household of four.
> - **Immigrants:** from January 1, 2027, the immigrants who can get savings are green card holders, Cuban or Haitian entrants, and COFA migrants.
> - **Taxes:** starting with tax year 2026, you repay all excess savings. The old repayment caps are gone.
> - **Phone:** 1-800-318-2596, with interpreters available.

## The dates to remember

From the [HealthCare.gov calendar](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/):

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| November 1, 2026 | Open Enrollment begins |
| December 15, 2026 | Last day to enroll or change plans for January 1, 2027 coverage |
| January 1, 2027 | Coverage starts if you enrolled by December 15 and paid your first premium |
| January 15, 2027 | Open Enrollment ends |
| February 1, 2027 | Coverage starts if you enrolled from December 16 to January 15 and paid your first premium |

Some states run their own Marketplace and may use a different website and different dates. Oregon, for example, uses ExploreHealthOR.gov. [Pick your state on HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/marketplace-in-your-state/) to find where you apply.

After this window closes, you can buy a plan only if you have a qualifying life event (see below). Medicaid and CHIP (coverage for children) accept applications all year.

## Who can buy, and who gets savings?

Buying a plan and getting savings are two separate questions. Many people with lawful immigration status can buy a plan but pay the full price.

According to the [HealthCare.gov page for immigrants](https://www.healthcare.gov/immigrants/lawfully-present-immigrants/), a law change means that starting January 1, 2027, these are the immigrants who can get Marketplace savings:

- Lawful permanent residents (green card holders)
- Cuban or Haitian entrants
- Compact of Free Association (COFA) migrants

Other "lawfully present" statuses, such as refugees, asylees, Temporary Protected Status holders and people on valid non-immigrant visas, may still be able to buy a plan **without savings**. If you get savings this year under one of those statuses, update your application and review your 2027 price before December 15.

If you plan to apply for a green card or visa, read up on the public charge rule. HealthCare.gov advises reviewing USCIS guidance before applying for Marketplace, Medicaid or CHIP coverage. Take questions about your own immigration case to a licensed immigration attorney.

## 2027 savings: what income qualifies?

According to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit), household income generally has to be between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level to get the premium tax credit, which lowers your monthly premium. Congress temporarily removed the 400% ceiling for 2021 through 2025. HealthCare.gov says the extra pandemic-era savings [ended on December 31, 2025](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/).

The table below uses the [2026 poverty figures from HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/federal-poverty-level-fpl/), the figures used for next year's plans. They match the [USCIS 400% table](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/filing-fees/poverty-guidelines) for the 48 contiguous states. Alaska and Hawaii have their own figures.

| Household size | 100% of poverty level | 400% of poverty level |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15,960 | $63,840 |
| 2 | $21,640 | $86,560 |
| 4 | $33,000 | $132,000 |

You enter your **expected 2027 income**, not last year's income. If you go over the 400% limit, even by a small amount, you generally lose the whole credit for that year. If you are self-employed, work in a nail salon, drive for an app or earn seasonal income, estimate carefully and update your application as soon as your income changes.

If your income is below 100% of the poverty level and your state has not expanded Medicaid, [HealthCare.gov says](https://www.healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/medicaid-expansion-and-you/) you won't qualify for either savings program. [Texas has not adopted expansion](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/state-indicator/state-activity-around-expanding-medicaid-under-the-affordable-care-act/) as of October 2026.

## Tax time: where surprises happen

Your monthly savings are an advance based on the income you estimated. Early the next year, the Marketplace sends you Form 1095-A, and you use Form 8962 to reconcile the credit on your tax return.

According to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/repayment-caps-eliminated-for-excess-advance-payments-of-the-premium-tax-credit), starting with **tax year 2026** you must repay the **full** excess amount. The repayment caps from earlier years no longer apply. The IRS subtracts the excess from your refund or adds it to your balance due.

If your real income beats your estimate, the bill can be large. If it goes over 400%, you may have to repay all the credit you received for the year. Keep Form 1095-A with your 2026 tax papers. See [Filing Taxes for Tax Year 2026](/en/blog/file-taxes-tax-year-2026).

## Choosing a plan: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum

The four levels differ in how you and the plan split your medical costs, based on [HealthCare.gov estimates](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/plans-categories/):

| Level | Plan pays about | You pay about |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | 60% | 40% |
| Silver | 70% | 30% |
| Silver with extra savings | 73–94% | 6–27% |
| Gold | 80% | 20% |
| Platinum | 90% | 10% |

The level says nothing about the quality of the doctors. A lower monthly premium usually comes with higher costs when you get care. The extra savings, called cost-sharing reductions, go only to people who qualify by income and choose Silver.

Check four things on every plan before you choose:

1. Whether your doctors and hospitals are in the network.
2. Whether the plan covers your prescriptions.
3. The deductible, which is the amount you pay before the plan starts sharing costs.
4. The out-of-pocket maximum for the year.

Catastrophic plans are open only to people under 30 or people with a hardship or affordability exemption.

## Already covered? Do you need to do anything?

Yes. HealthCare.gov says that if you do nothing, [you may be re-enrolled automatically](https://www.healthcare.gov/keep-or-change-plan/), but some people won't be, and your plan may change. Without an update, the Marketplace estimates your income from its most recent data, so your savings may come out wrong and you may repay the difference at tax time. Prices and plan lists also change every year.

Before December 15, log in, update your income, household size and address, then compare plans.

## What if you miss the deadline?

After January 15, 2027, you need a [qualifying life event](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/) to buy a plan during the year (a Special Enrollment Period):

- You lost coverage in the past 60 days or expect to lose it in the next 60 days. For Medicaid or CHIP, the window is the past 90 days.
- You got married. Pick a plan by the end of the month, and coverage can start the first day of the next month.
- You had a baby or adopted a child. Coverage can start on the day of the event if you enroll within 60 days. See [Having a Baby in the U.S.](/en/blog/having-a-baby-in-the-us-paperwork).
- You moved to a new ZIP code or county, or moved to the U.S. from abroad.
- You became a U.S. citizen, or had certain other events.

You may have to send documents. Without acceptable documents, you won't qualify. Dropping COBRA voluntarily doesn't count.

## Age 65 and older: Medicare

If you have Medicare, you generally can't buy a Marketplace plan. [HealthCare.gov says](https://www.healthcare.gov/medicare/medicare-and-the-marketplace/) it is against the law for someone who knows you have Medicare to sell you a Marketplace plan, even if you have only Part A or only Part B. The exception is for people who pay a premium for Part A, and switching can trigger late enrollment penalties for Part B and Part D.

Medicare has its own open enrollment, separate from this one.

## Watch out for anyone who "signs you up"

CMS has reported [agents switching people's plans without their knowledge](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-statement-agent-broker-marketplace-activity-update). Before you let someone help you:

- Share sensitive information only on official, secure websites.
- Keep your own HealthCare.gov login and password.
- Write down the helper's name, and ask whether they hold a health insurance license in your state.
- Check the income on your application yourself. Inflating income to reach a savings level or listing the wrong household size is a false statement, and you are the one who signs the application and answers for it at tax time.
- If your plan changes unexpectedly, call 1-800-318-2596 right away.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does Open Enrollment 2027 start and end?

On HealthCare.gov, it runs from November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15, 2026 for coverage that starts January 1, 2027. States with their own Marketplace may use different dates.

### I've had my green card for less than 5 years. Can I buy a plan?

HealthCare.gov says that during the 5-year waiting period for Medicaid and CHIP, you may still get Marketplace coverage if you are otherwise eligible. Starting January 1, 2027, green card holders remain in the group that can get savings if their income qualifies.

### Can refugees or TPS holders still get savings in 2027?

According to HealthCare.gov, from January 1, 2027, savings for immigrants go to green card holders, Cuban or Haitian entrants, and COFA migrants. Other statuses may be able to buy a plan at full price.

### What income qualifies for savings in 2027?

Generally 100% to 400% of the federal poverty level. In the 48 contiguous states, that is $15,960 to $63,840 for one person and $33,000 to $132,000 for a household of four.

### Will my plan renew if I do nothing?

Many people are re-enrolled automatically, but not everyone, and your savings may no longer match your income. Log in, update your application and compare plans by December 15.

### What happens if I report the wrong income?

You reconcile your savings on Form 8962 when you file taxes. Starting with tax year 2026, if you received too much, you repay the full excess because the repayment caps are gone.

### Can I still buy a plan after January 15, 2027?

Only with a qualifying life event, such as losing coverage, getting married, having a baby or moving. Medicaid and CHIP accept applications all year.
